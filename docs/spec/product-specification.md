# **DepartureDesk Product Specification**

*Consolidated edition, October 2026*

## **About this specification**

This is the single source of truth for DepartureDesk's product design and requirements. It replaces the Ideal Model Working Draft, the Structured Requirements Draft, the Product Foundation Draft, the Product Gap Inventory and the area drafts written in October 2026, all of which are now archived.  
Each product area is a tab in this document. This first tab holds the front matter, the product overview and the glossary. The last tabs hold the decision log and the questions for the agency's advisers.

### **How each area is organized**

> * **Context:** what the area covers and why, in plain language.  
> * **Rules:** each rule has an ID, a status and one statement of the rule. A rule appears in exactly one place; anywhere else that depends on it cites the ID.  
> * **Tables:** lifecycle tables for each record that has states, and workflow matrices for events that change several records at once. Every row cites the rules it comes from.  
> * **Scenarios:** Given/When/Then cases that cite the rule IDs they test.  
> * **Open questions:** decisions still needed in that area.

### **Status key**

> * **Agreed: a current product decision. Agreement does not by itself mean the rule is required in MVP.**  
> * **Detail:** the behavior is agreed, but its exact configuration or interface is still to be defined.  
> * **Proposed:** a suggested rule awaiting approval.  
> * **Open:** a product or policy decision is still needed.  
> * **Review:** needs confirmation from the agency's accountant or legal adviser.  
> * **Deferred:** intentionally outside MVP.  
> * **Retired:** no longer in force; kept with a pointer to the decision log entry that retired it.

### **IDs**

Rules use the area prefix and a two-digit number, such as ACC-07. Scenarios use SC, the area prefix and a number, such as SC-ACC-03. Numbers are never reused.

### **Changing a rule**

Edit the rule in its area, add a decision log entry naming the rule ID, and search the specification for the ID to update anything that cites it. Never copy rule text into another area.

## **Product overview**

### **Context**

DepartureDesk helps a travel agency run group Departures and individual Client Trips, from supplier commitment through booking, travel and financial closeout. It connects three views of the same business:

> * **Supplier commitments and inventory:** what the agency can sell or has agreed to buy, and on what terms.  
> * **Client Trips and bookings:** what each client is buying, for which travelers, and how each service is fulfilled.  
> * **Money:** what clients owe, what the agency owes suppliers, what suppliers owe the agency, and who actually handled each payment.

These views connect through explicit records and links. A client booking may use shared group inventory, or it may be a one-off supplier reservation with no shared pool; the money rules work either way. Client purchases, supplier arrangements, inventory, supplier reservations and payments stay distinct records, linked where work or reporting needs them.

### **Audience**

| Audience | What they use DepartureDesk for |
| :---- | :---- |
| Agency leadership and administrators | Set up the agency, define defaults, manage staff access, and monitor Departures, money and open work |
| Agency operations and booking staff | Build Departures and supplier arrangements, sell and service Client Trips and bookings, and manage supplier follow-up and documents |
| Agency finance staff | Record client and supplier money, credits, refunds and commission, and reconcile Departures |
| Authorized resellers | Sell offers published to them and look after the bookings assigned to them |
| Clients and travelers | Receive optional booking summaries, confirmations, invoices, receipts, statements and trip documents from the agency or an assigned reseller |

Access for each audience is defined in Users and access.

### **Rules**

**OVR-01 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk is an operational system. It records balances, obligations, settlements and operational history, and reports trip profitability. It is not the agency's accounting system and does not decide tax, revenue-recognition or trust treatment (RPT-17).  
**OVR-02 · Agreed.** In MVP, a Client Trip links to zero or one Departure. A Client Trip with no Departure may hold one-off services.  
**OVR-03 · Agreed.** Supplier integrations are not required in MVP. Staff record supplier requests, responses, commitments and settlements manually.  
**OVR-04 · Agreed.** The agency, not the system, decides whether a Departure is Ready to sell and whether it proceeds, is postponed or is cancelled. The system makes no automatic viability assessment.  
Clients have no login in MVP (ACC-19).

## **Glossary**

Use these terms precisely in requirements. The interface may use shorter wording where context is clear.

### **Terminology rules**

> * Write "client booking" and "supplier reservation" whenever both could be meant.  
> * Write "inventory allocation" and "payment application" whenever either kind of allocation could be meant.  
> * Distinguish traveler assignment within a unit from the supplier's physical assignment.  
> * Do not use "trip," "booking," "reservation," "allocation" or "commission" without enough context to tell which is meant.  
> * "Travel party" may be used in ordinary prose for a group of travelers, but it names no record.  
> * "Client contact" means a contact point on a client or traveler profile. The agency profile field is "agency contact name" (SET-04).

# **Users and access**

Rule prefix: ACC.

## **Context**

Every user other than a Platform Administrator belongs to one agency, and an agency's data is separated from every other agency's. Within an agency, every staff user can do everyday booking work, and five predefined permissions add Departure management, supplier arrangements, financial records, passport access, and reseller relationships. Permissions apply across the whole agency; assigning someone to a Departure routes work to them but neither grants nor limits access. Supplier costs, commission, and margin are visible only to staff whose permissions involve them.  
Resellers see only what is published to them and the bookings assigned to them. Clients do not use the application in MVP. Platform Administrators run DepartureDesk itself and have no standing access to any agency's operational data. Every committed business change is recorded in an audit trail that only Agency Admins can view.

## **Rules**

### **Identity and tenancy**

**ACC-01 · Agreed.** Platform Administrator accounts operate across agencies without an agency membership. They create and maintain Agency account and setup records, and activate or inactivate agencies. They sign in through a separate path that uses no agency code.  
**ACC-02 · Agreed.** Every other user, including agency staff and reseller users, belongs to exactly one agency in MVP. A person who works with several agencies needs a separate account for each.  
**ACC-03 · Agreed.** Agency-scoped sign-in uses a unique, stable, non-secret agency code. Invitations and agency-specific login links include the code; generic sign-in asks for it and confirms the agency name. The code routes sign-in and grants no access.  
**ACC-04 · Agreed.** A signed-in agency user can access only their own agency's records, and only the actions their user type and permissions allow.

### **User types and permissions**

**ACC-05 · Agreed.** Agencies use built-in user types and a small set of predefined permissions. Agencies cannot create custom roles or permission definitions.  
**ACC-06 · Agreed.** When a Platform Administrator creates an agency, they designate and invite its initial Agency Admin. An Agency Admin may invite agency staff, assign predefined permissions, resend or revoke pending invitations, and deactivate users.  
**ACC-07 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin always holds every predefined permission and has authority over agency users, agency-wide settings, Departures, supplier commitments, financial records, and reseller relationships.  
**ACC-08 · Agreed.** Each predefined permission applies across the whole agency, never to particular Departures, offices, or clients.  
**ACC-09 · Agreed.** Assigning staff to a Departure team routes work and responsibility only. It does not grant access, restrict access, or create a separate permission.  
**ACC-30 · Agreed.** Every agency staff user, with no permission, may create and manage Client Trips, client and traveler profiles (without passport details), bookings, holds, proposals, acceptances, and confirmations; make amendments and cancellations, except entering their money effects; issue and send documents; use the communication log, tasks, and incidents; and view Departures and supplier arrangements without supplier costs, commission, or margin.  
**ACC-11 · Agreed.** Beyond the baseline in ACC-30, staff may hold any of five predefined permissions, listed in the permissions table.  
**ACC-31 · Agreed.** Supplier costs, commission, and margin are visible only to staff with the Departure management, Supplier arrangements, or Financial records permission. Other staff see client prices only, including on the Departure summary (OPS-30).  
**ACC-10 · Agreed.** Changing supplier commitments requires the Supplier arrangements permission, and entering or changing client or supplier financial records requires the Financial records permission. Refunds and financial adjustments are always staff actions.  
**ACC-32 · Agreed.** Amendments to a confirmed booking's price, including discounts and change-fee waivers (BKG-26, BKG-35), are baseline work. Entering their money effects, such as a refund due, needs the Financial records permission.  
**ACC-33 · Agreed.** Only an Agency Admin may manage users, permissions, and work groups; change agency settings and defaults (SET-13); view audit history and run the full data export (RPT-13, RPT-16); and import clients and travelers (CLT-37).

### **The last Agency Admin**

**ACC-12 · Agreed.** The system refuses to deactivate, demote, or remove the last active Agency Admin, including an admin removing themselves. The setup checklist (SET-15) warns while an agency has only one Agency Admin.  
**ACC-34 · Agreed.** If an agency's only Agency Admin is unavailable, the agency's primary account contact (SET-02) may ask DepartureDesk to designate a new one. A Platform Administrator verifies the request and designates the new Agency Admin. This grants the Platform Administrator no access to agency data (ACC-20); it is logged, and the primary contact and any previous Agency Admin are notified.

### **Resellers**

**ACC-13 · Agreed.** Agency Admins, or staff with the Reseller relationships permission, may authorize, invite, reactivate, pause, suspend, or end a reseller relationship, and set whether the agency may send documents directly to that reseller's clients (DOC-08). Assigning a booking to a reseller does not authorize the relationship.  
**ACC-14 · Agreed.** A reseller user sees only offers published to its channel and bookings currently assigned to it. It never sees agency-owned or unassigned bookings.  
**ACC-15 · Agreed.** For bookings assigned to it, a reseller user may place holds against published availability, confirm a held booking after the client accepts the published price and terms (subject to DEP-10), view charges and balances, send system-generated client documents, request amendments and cancellations, and discard an unconfirmed booking while its hold is active (BKG-56). A reseller may give staff the details of a payment already made, but cannot enter or change financial records, issue refunds, change supplier commitments, run reports, or export data (RPT-05).  
**ACC-16 · Agreed.** A reseller relationship may be Booking paused or fully suspended. Booking paused blocks new holds and bookings but keeps access to assigned bookings; an agency setting controls whether active holds may still be confirmed, defaulting to allow confirmation until normal hold expiry. Full suspension blocks sign-in.  
**ACC-17 · Agreed.** While a reseller relationship is suspended, its bookings stay assigned and the reseller cannot sign in. Notifications to the reseller stop. Payment reminders and document defaults go to the client if the relationship allows direct contact (DOC-08); otherwise staff get a task to handle the client.  
**ACC-35 · Agreed.** When a reseller relationship ends, staff reassign each of its bookings to agency ownership or another reseller. Until reassigned, each booking shows a "Reseller relationship ended" condition in Needs attention. Ending a relationship never changes the seller of record, and seller commission already owed stays payable unless staff adjust it (FIN-05).

### **Booking ownership**

**ACC-18 · Agreed.** Booking creator, booking owner, and seller of record are separate facts. Staff with the Departure management permission may assign a booking to an authorized reseller or to agency ownership. Reassignment changes reseller access and operational notices only: notify the current and new owner, route agency-owned work to the relevant staff queue, and leave the seller of record and seller commission unchanged.

### **Clients**

**ACC-19 · Agreed.** Clients have no login, self-service, checkout, or payment entry in MVP. They receive documents and communications from agency staff or the assigned reseller.

### **Platform Administrator access to agency data**

**ACC-20 · Agreed.** Platform Administrators have no standing access to client, traveler, booking, supplier, or financial records. Maintaining Agency account and setup records does not grant that access.  
**ACC-21 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin may approve a time-limited access grant for a Platform Administrator, scoped to that agency and to specific data and actions. Viewing and changing require separate scopes, and passport details require their own scope. The grant records the requester, approving Agency Admin, reason, and duration. There is no emergency access that bypasses agency approval.  
**ACC-22 · Agreed.** A Platform Administrator requests an access grant with a reason, and an Agency Admin approves or rejects it. Scopes are chosen separately for viewing and for changing: bookings and profiles, financial records, and supplier records; passport details are a separate, view-only scope. A grant lasts up to 7 days by default, or up to 30 days if the Agency Admin allows, expires automatically, and can be revoked at any time. While it is active, the Platform Administrator sees a banner, every action is logged (ACC-28), and the agency's admins can see the grant.

### **Audit**

**ACC-23 · Agreed.** Keep an append-only audit trail of committed business changes to bookings, travelers, and services; supplier commitments; assignments, amendments, transfers, and cancellations; financial records; agency and user access; reseller relationships; agency defaults; and exports (RPT-14).  
**ACC-24 · Agreed.** Record one audit event per action, with the actor (including system processes), agency, time, affected record, action, and only the relevant changed fields or before-and-after values. Link to the authoritative record rather than copying it. Preserve before-and-after amounts for financial changes.  
**ACC-25 · Agreed.** Do not audit routine interface activity (draft keystrokes, autosaves, validation failures, searches, filters, ordinary record views) or technical retries and polling. Keep technical diagnostics separate from the audit trail.  
**ACC-26 · Agreed.** Require a reason for overrides and exceptions, financial corrections, Platform Administrator access grants, and changes to financial or cancellation defaults. Routine profile and document-footer changes need no reason.  
**ACC-27 · Agreed.** Log every staff and Platform Administrator view, download, export, and supplier share of passport records, including the recipient of a share. Never copy passport numbers or image contents into an audit event.  
**ACC-28 · Agreed.** Log each Platform Administrator change to Agency account and setup records with the administrator, agency, action, and time. Under an access grant, log the grant details and every view, download, or change made under it.  
**ACC-29 · Agreed.** Only Agency Admins may view or export their agency's audit history (RPT-16). Audit events are kept for the life of the agency account.  
**ACC-36 · Review.** How long audit events are kept after an agency account closes, and how they are then deleted, follows a retention policy reviewed by the agency's legal adviser.

## **Scenarios**

**SC-ACC-01.** Given a user is invited to an agency, when they sign in, then they can access only that agency's records and the actions their permissions allow. Tests ACC-02, ACC-04.  
**SC-ACC-02.** Given an Agency Admin grants a staff member the Departure management permission, when that staff member opens a Departure outside their assigned work queue, then they may manage it; queue assignment neither grants nor removes access. Tests ACC-08, ACC-09.  
**SC-ACC-03.** Given a booking assigned to reseller A with a seller of record, when staff reassign it to reseller B, then A loses access, B gains access, both receive the operational notice, and the seller of record and seller commission are unchanged. Tests ACC-14, ACC-18.  
**SC-ACC-04.** Given a booking reassigned to agency ownership, when a reseller user searches for it, then it is not visible to any reseller. Tests ACC-14, ACC-18.  
**SC-ACC-05.** Given a reseller relationship is Booking paused with the default setting, when the reseller tries to place a new hold, then it is blocked; when the reseller confirms an existing active hold before expiry, then confirmation is allowed. Tests ACC-16.  
**SC-ACC-06.** Given a Platform Administrator with no access grant, when they try to open a booking in an agency, then access is refused, even though they can edit that agency's setup record. Tests ACC-20.  
**SC-ACC-07.** Given an approved grant to view booking records, when the Platform Administrator opens a passport record, then access is refused because passport details need their own scope; when they open a booking, then the view is logged under the grant. Tests ACC-21, ACC-28.  
**SC-ACC-08.** Given a staff member downloads a traveler's passport image, when the audit event is written, then it records the actor, time, record, and action, but not the passport number or image. Tests ACC-27.  
**SC-ACC-09.** Given a booking agent with no permissions, when they open a Departure, then they see client prices but no supplier costs, commission, or margin. Tests ACC-30, ACC-31.  
**SC-ACC-10.** Given a booking agent who agrees a \$200 discount with a client on a confirmed booking, then they can record the amendment; recording the resulting refund due requires a colleague with the Financial records permission. Tests ACC-32.  
**SC-ACC-11.** Given an agency with one Agency Admin, when that admin tries to remove their own Agency Admin role, then it is refused and the setup checklist warns about having only one admin. Tests ACC-12.  
**SC-ACC-12.** Given an agency whose only Agency Admin has left, when the primary account contact asks DepartureDesk for help, then a Platform Administrator designates a new Agency Admin after verification, without gaining access to any booking or financial record. Tests ACC-34.  
**SC-ACC-13.** Given a reseller relationship that ends with 12 assigned bookings, then each booking shows "Reseller relationship ended" in Needs attention until staff reassign it, and the seller commission owed to the reseller is unchanged. Tests ACC-35.  
**SC-ACC-14.** Given an approved 7-day grant to view financial records, when 7 days pass, then the grant expires automatically; every view during it is logged. Tests ACC-22.

# **Agency setup**

Rule prefix: SET.

## **Context**

An agency account is the boundary for everything an agency does in DepartureDesk. Setup establishes the agency's identity on client documents, its staff, and the defaults that govern day-to-day behavior. Only Agency Admins change agency defaults, and a change never alters terms a client has already accepted.  
This area holds the agency account, its status, its client-facing profile and document settings, time zone and offices, and the rules for changing defaults. Each default is defined in the area that uses it; the defaults table lists them all in one place.

## **Rules**

### **Agency account and status**

**SET-01 · Agreed.** The agency account is the tenant boundary for staff, settings, Departures, Client Trips, supplier arrangements, reseller relationships, documents, and financial records.  
**SET-02 · Agreed.** The platform-administered agency directory stores the agency code, a required primary account contact name and email, and an optional phone number, for DepartureDesk notices and administration. These details never appear on client documents. The primary account contact is also the route for recovering the agency if its only Agency Admin becomes unavailable (ACC-34).  
**SET-03 · Agreed.** A Platform Administrator may set an agency Inactive. Inactive blocks sign-in for all agency staff and reseller users and blocks new operational activity, while preserving bookings, financial history, and audit records without deleting or transferring them. Reactivation restores access under existing permissions. Inactivation never grants a Platform Administrator access to operational records.  
**SET-16 · Agreed.** An agency may be set to Sales paused, separate from Inactive. Sales paused blocks new holds, bookings, and publishing across every Departure, while existing bookings, payments, and servicing continue. An Agency Admin may set it for business reasons, and a Platform Administrator for account reasons such as a lapsed registration. Each pause records who set it and why, and reseller users receive a notice. While it applies, no Departure is On sale (DEP-05).

### **Agency profile and document settings**

**SET-04 · Agreed.** Agency Admins maintain the client-facing agency profile. The display name and contact email are required to generate client documents; they do not gate agency activation or Departure readiness. Optional fields are agency contact name, legal or business name, phone, mailing address, and seller-of-travel disclosure.  
**SET-05 · Agreed.** The seller-of-travel disclosure is one multiline text field. Preserve its line breaks, omit it when blank, and do not validate its legal sufficiency.  
**SET-06 · Agreed.** Each profile field has an inclusion flag for each of confirmations, invoices, receipts, and statements. Defaults: display name and contact email on all four; phone on all four when provided; legal or business name and mailing address on invoices, receipts, and statements; agency contact name and seller-of-travel disclosure off until the agency selects them.  
**SET-07 · Agreed.** Provide a confirmation footer, shown on confirmations by default, and a financial-document footer shared by invoices, receipts, and statements and shown on all three by default. Footers accept limited Markdown: paragraphs and line breaks, bold and italic, bulleted and numbered lists, and web links; no headings, tables, images, embedded content, or raw HTML. Provide an editor preview, and render formatted text on documents.  
**SET-08 · Agreed.** An agency may upload an optional logo, shown on its documents (DOC-51). MVP requires no tax-display fields, tax identifiers, payment processor credentials, or other branded assets. Add tax-display fields only if an agency's accountant or tax advisor identifies a need and confirms their placement.

### **Time zone and offices**

**SET-09 · Agreed.** Each agency has one time zone, stored as an IANA identifier rather than a fixed offset, shared by all offices. Store instants such as payments and audit events in UTC and display them in the agency time zone. Keep date-only facts as dates.  
**SET-10 · Agreed.** A date-only deadline resolves to 11:59 p.m. agency local time and keeps both its governing local date, time, and zone and its resolved instant. Timed service occurrences keep their local date and time and an explicit IANA zone, defaulted from the Departure. All-day travel dates stay date-only.  
**SET-11 · Agreed.** Changing the agency or Departure time-zone default affects future defaults only. It does not reinterpret existing service occurrences, confirmed records, issued documents, or resolved deadlines.  
**SET-12 · Agreed.** Agencies may define optional Office records for work assignment, responsibility, filtering, and reporting. Offices are not tenants, data owners, or access boundaries, and all offices use the agency time zone.

### **Defaults and change control**

**SET-13 · Agreed.** Only an Agency Admin may create or change agency defaults. A Departure may override a default only where the defining rule says an override exists.  
**SET-14 · Agreed.** A change to an agency default governs new or explicitly amended work only. Accepted prices, terms, fees, and cancellation schedules stay attached to each booking unless its accepted terms authorize a change or the client accepts an amendment.  
**SET-15 · Agreed.** Provide an optional, dismissible setup checklist for Agency Admins, linking to profile, access, time zone, reminder, document, and numbering settings. It warns while the agency has only one Agency Admin (ACC-12). Completing it does not gate agency activation or Ready to sell.

# **References**

Rule prefix: REF.

## **Context**

People refer to records by a reference whenever they speak or write about one outside the app: on the phone, in an email subject, on a printed document, or to a supplier or bookkeeper. Records that are only ever reached through their parent on screen, such as allocations or installments, have no reference.  
A reference is a type prefix and a number unique within the agency. It never changes and is never reused, so a reference written on a receipt five years ago still finds the same record. One search box finds any record by its reference, by number alone across all types, or by a supplier's or previous system's reference.

## **Rules**

### **Which records have references**

**REF-01 · Agreed.** The records in the references table have a reference. Shells, allocations, charge lines, installments, tasks, conditions, review items and notifications do not; they are reached through their parent record.  
**REF-02 · Agreed.** A reference is a fixed prefix for the record type, a hyphen, and a number of digits only, shown with leading zeros to a minimum width for that type. Agencies cannot change the prefixes in MVP.  
**REF-03 · Agreed.** A reference is unique within the agency for its type. It never changes and is never reused, even when its record is discarded, cancelled, inactivated or superseded.  
**REF-04 · Agreed.** A client booking's reference is its Client Trip's reference followed by a sequence number within the trip, such as T-24817-2, so a booking always shows its trip.  
**REF-05 · Agreed.** An issued document's reference is its type prefix and number. A reissued version keeps the number with a version suffix, such as INV-01884-v2 (DOC-12). Invoice and receipt numbers run without gaps within the agency; other sequences may have gaps.  
**REF-06 · Agreed.** A Departure may also have an agency short code, such as MED27, unique within the agency. Unlike its reference, the short code may be changed.

### **Numbering**

**REF-07 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin may set the starting number for each sequence before the first record of that type is created, so an agency moving from another system can continue its numbering. Once a record of that type exists, the starting number is fixed.  
**REF-08 · Agreed.** An imported client or traveler keeps its reference from the previous system as an alternate reference, which is searchable but never replaces its DepartureDesk reference (CLT-37).  
**REF-09 · Agreed.** Supplier references, such as group, confirmation, reservation and invoice numbers, are stored beside the agency's references and never in their place.  
**REF-10 · Agreed.** References and short codes are not secrets. Knowing one never grants access to a record (as with the agency code, ACC-03).

### **Search**

**REF-11 · Agreed.** One search box finds records by reference. Results are limited to records the user may see; a reseller finds only records on bookings assigned to it.  
**REF-12 · Agreed.** A search with a prefix, such as T-24817, searches only that record type. Matching ignores letter case and a missing hyphen. If exactly one record matches, it opens directly.  
**REF-13 · Agreed.** A search by number alone, such as 24817, lists every record of any type with that number, each labelled by its type. Leading zeros are ignored. If exactly one record matches, it opens directly.  
**REF-14 · Agreed.** A Client Trip result also lists the trip's bookings, and a search including the booking sequence, such as 24817-2, finds that booking.  
**REF-15 · Agreed.** A document reference without a version suffix lists every version of that document, latest first; with a suffix, it opens that version.  
**REF-16 · Agreed.** A number-only search also matches alternate references from imports (REF-08) and supplier references (REF-09), each labelled as such.  
**REF-17 · Agreed.** Departure short codes and names can also be searched as text.  
**REF-18 · Deferred.** Agency-defined prefixes and reference formats.

# **Suppliers and arrangements**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the Ideal Model, the Structured Requirements, or our discussions of 28 October. **Deferred** marks items outside MVP. Rule prefix: **SUP**.

## **Context**

A supplier is anyone the agency buys from. A supplier agreement holds that supplier's standing terms, which seed each new arrangement. A supplier arrangement is the agency's commitment with a supplier for a service on one Departure: what space or service the supplier is holding, on what terms, at what cost, by which deadlines, and with which group benefits. Arrangements are where supplier risk lives. They tell staff what the agency has promised to buy, what it can sell, and what must happen by when.

This area defines suppliers, agreements, arrangements and their terms. Inventory groups, shells and pools are defined in Inventory; supplier money (payables, commission received, credits, settlements) in Supplier money; task mechanics in Operations.

## **Rules**

### **Suppliers**

**SUP-01 · Agreed.** A supplier record holds the supplier's name, the service categories it provides, contacts (name, role, email, phone), and notes.

**SUP-02 · Agreed.** A supplier has one or more payee accounts. Each payee account has a name, currency, and payment instructions as free text. Supplier payments, credits and commission settle against a payee account, and a supplier credit can be applied only within the same supplier and payee account (SMN-44). Structured bank account fields are deferred.

**SUP-34 · Agreed.** Changing a payee account's payment instructions requires a reason and is audited. The next payment recorded to that payee shows a warning that its payment instructions recently changed, so staff can confirm the change with the supplier before paying.

**SUP-03 · Deferred.** A reusable catalog of supplier products such as ships, hotels and routes. In MVP, staff enter the ship, hotel or route on the arrangement.

### **Supplier agreements**

**SUP-04 · Agreed.** Supplier-wide terms can be stored once as a supplier agreement and copied into a new arrangement as starting values. Staff may override them on the arrangement. Later edits to the agreement do not change existing arrangements.

**SUP-05 · Agreed.** Supplier quotes and agreement documents are kept with the arrangement as evidence. Do not add a separate source field to each rate. Whether an amount is an estimate or supplier-confirmed is recorded separately from the evidence.

### **Supplier arrangements**

**SUP-06 · Agreed.** An arrangement records the supplier, the covered services and dates, the group contract or confirmation reference, documents, deadlines, costs, commercial terms, and group benefits. The group reference stays on the arrangement, separate from supplier reservation references and unit assignments.

**SUP-07 · Agreed.** An arrangement belongs to exactly one Departure. A standalone supplier reservation with no Departure carries its own terms and cost on the reservation (see Bookings and packages). Allotments shared across several Departures are deferred.

**SUP-08 · Agreed.** One arrangement may cover several inventory groups (see Inventory).

**SUP-09 · Agreed.** Supplier costs and rates belong to the arrangement. Client prices belong to offerings and are maintained separately (see Bookings and packages).

**SUP-10 · Agreed.** Each arrangement states whether supplier reservations are shared across the arrangement, tracked per sellable unit, or not tracked below the arrangement. Category-level defaults are not required in MVP.

### **Arrangement lifecycle**

**SUP-11 · Agreed.** An arrangement is Draft, Confirmed, Closed or Cancelled, with the transitions in the arrangement lifecycle table below. A Confirmed arrangement closes when its Departure is Financially reconciled (DEP-46); a cancelled arrangement may end at any time before then.

**SUP-12 · Agreed.** Unit shells and capacity pools are created when the arrangement is confirmed, not while it is a draft.

**SUP-13 · Agreed.** Once confirmed supplier terms create a contractual commitment, record it as committed cost exposure even when nothing is due yet. Forecasts that are not committed stay separate (see Supplier money).

**SUP-14 · Agreed.** After confirmation, changes to capacity, terms or cost are dated adjustments that preserve the original records. A supplier amendment adjusts the current obligation without rewriting past due events or settlements.

### **Commitment terms**

**SUP-15 · Agreed.** Each inventory group records an availability promise, defaulting from its arrangement: Firm block, Releasable block, Courtesy or option, On request, or Rate only. One arrangement can therefore mix firm space with space held on request. The promise drives behavior: On request and Rate only show no available balance (INV-04), and a hold against Courtesy or option space is conditional (INV-22).

**SUP-16 · Agreed.** Each arrangement records an obligation shape: Pay per unit used, Committed quantity, Fixed charge for a whole resource, Minimum pickup, or Mixed. Minimum pickup and Mixed allow a count-change threshold (SUP-26).

**SUP-17 · Agreed.** Deposit schedules, minimums, release dates, attrition rules, cancellation terms and payment deadlines are recorded on the arrangement, or on the category or inventory group they apply to. They are never confused with inventory movements or settlements.

**SUP-18 · Agreed.** These terms are structured fields: deposit and payment schedule lines, cancellation tiers, typed deadlines, minimum and pickup thresholds, the commission rate and expected amount, and group benefits. Everything else, including attrition formulas and special conditions, is recorded as plain-language terms with attached documents.

**SUP-19 · Agreed.** Staff explicitly select which supplier terms apply to an arrangement or booking and record the controlling date: the date the supplier's terms measure from, such as the sailing date or the date the deposit was paid. It decides which terms or promotion apply and where a booking falls in the cancellation tiers; for example, a "book by 31 March" promotion is measured from the booking or deposit date, while cancellation tiers count back from sailing. Terms never switch automatically when dates change; a change that affects which terms apply creates a review item (DEP-12).

**SUP-20 · Agreed.** A supplier cancellation tier records a cutoff, a percentage, a named calculation base, and its scope (service, category, unit or booking, as the contract defines). The applicable tier calculates a proposed supplier penalty for staff review; staff verify the supplier's actual outcome before posting an obligation. Terms outside this structure are entered manually. Attrition and multi-factor penalty formulas are deferred.

**SUP-21 · Agreed.** Commission terms are recorded from the supplier's stated terms. There is no universal commission base and no automatic earned-date rule. Staff may record supplier-stated rate components; automatically splitting rates into commissionable, non-commissionable, tax and fee parts is deferred.

### **Supplier deadlines**

**SUP-22 · Agreed.** Every supplier deadline has a type:

| Deadline type | Completed when | Task by default |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Option or release | The decision, supplier response and any inventory adjustment are recorded | Yes |
| Deposit or payment | The required amount is settled by payment, credit or commission offset | Yes |
| Names submission | Validated names are submitted and the reference recorded | Yes |
| Rooming list | The list is submitted and the reference recorded | Yes |
| Ticketing | Tickets are issued and recorded | Yes |
| Final count or manifest | The count is submitted and the supplier outcome recorded | Yes |
| Cancellation-tier cutoff | Not applicable | No, informational |
| Other | Staff record completion | Yes |

**SUP-23 · Agreed.** Each actionable supplier deadline creates a staff follow-up task as soon as it is recorded, whether the arrangement is Draft or Confirmed, and staff may mark a deadline informational-only. If a Draft arrangement is discarded, its open tasks are waived automatically with the reason "arrangement discarded". Task statuses, reminders and queues are defined in Operations.

**SUP-24 · Agreed.** A deadline keeps the supplier's stated cutoff time and time zone. A date-only deadline resolves as in SET-10.

**SUP-25 · Agreed.** A deadline prompts staff only. It never automatically releases inventory, submits information or pays a supplier.

### **Count-change warnings**

**SUP-26 · Agreed.** Staff may enter a minimum or pickup threshold from the supplier's terms for an arrangement or inventory group. When confirmed allocations cross it, show a warning on the Departure and in the Needs attention queue. Temporary holds and cancelled bookings do not count. The warning changes no status, price, benefit or penalty, and calculates no attrition.

**SUP-27 · Agreed.** When a count-change warning fires, staff review the group benefits recorded as expected for that arrangement.

### **Group benefits**

**SUP-28 · Agreed.** Benefit qualification terms are recorded in plain language. For a threshold-based benefit, staff enter the expected quantity or value, then later the supplier-confirmed qualifying count and earned benefit. The system does not calculate thresholds from bookings in MVP.

**SUP-29 · Agreed.** Track each benefit's expected, confirmed or earned, used or applied, and remaining quantity or value, visible at arrangement and Departure level. At closeout, staff may mark a remaining benefit used, expired or unearned, or carried forward, with a note or reference. This status creates no financial transaction.

**SUP-30 · Agreed.** A benefit may be kept by the agency as income or commission, applied to a client booking as an explicit price adjustment, or given as a nonfinancial inclusion on a client booking, assignable to specific travelers. A nonfinancial inclusion creates no charge, receivable or revenue entry.

**SUP-31 · Agreed.** A divisible benefit may be split across several uses where supplier terms allow, tracking each use and the remaining balance. An indivisible benefit, such as a comp room or upgrade, is assigned whole.

**SUP-32 · Agreed.** Where supplier follow-through matters, an inclusion is tracked as awaiting supplier confirmation, confirmed, or delivered, separately from its assignment. If a supplier fails to deliver, flag it for staff; any replacement or client credit is an amendment.

**SUP-33 · Agreed.** Staff may record an estimated value for a benefit, labeled projected and excluded from supplier balances, client prices and actual profitability. Actual financial impact is recorded separately with its amount and reference, linked to the related supplier cost, credit or settlement.

## **Arrangement lifecycle**

# **Inventory**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussions of 28 October. **Deferred** marks items outside MVP. Rule prefix: **INV**.

## **Context**

Inventory is what the agency can allocate to clients from its supplier arrangements. Some inventory is made of distinct resources that staff or the supplier need to identify, such as cabins, rooms and coaches; each is tracked as a unit shell. Other inventory is interchangeable until later, such as activity tickets or flight seats; it is tracked as a quantity in a capacity pool. Some services have no shared inventory at all and are booked directly with the supplier.

A client booking uses inventory through an inventory allocation, which moves from Held (a temporary hold) to Reserved (the agency's continuing allocation after the client confirms). A supplier reservation is a separate record that tracks whether the supplier has accepted a request. Neither status implies the other: Reserved means the agency has set the space aside for the client, not that the supplier has confirmed anything. Every change to inventory is recorded as an event, so staff can see both the current balance and how it got there.

Arrangements and their terms are defined in Suppliers and arrangements. Hold duration, extension, package holds, client booking states and cancellation flows are defined in Bookings and packages. Capacity conflicts caused by a postponement are defined in Departures.

## **Rules**

### **Inventory groups**

**INV-01 · Agreed.** An inventory group is the smallest part of a supplier arrangement with its own availability and allocation rules. Start a separate group when availability is committed, tracked or allocated differently, for example by room type, date range, flight, class, transportation segment or activity session.

**INV-02 · Agreed.** Two inventory groups share capacity only when the same supplier commitment and capacity rules govern them. Split a group when dates, availability, eligibility, release terms or other supplier rules change what staff can safely allocate or release.

**INV-03 · Agreed.** Each inventory group uses one representation: unit shells, a capacity pool, or no shared inventory (direct supplier booking). The service category suggests a default, shown in the table under INV-12; staff choose the actual representation for each group.

**INV-04 · Agreed.** On request and Rate only are availability promises (SUP-15), not representations. A group with either promise shows no numeric available balance.

### **Unit shells**

**INV-05 · Agreed.** A unit shell is one independently tracked, supplier-reservable sellable resource, such as a cabin, room, vehicle, seat or ticket. It is never a client booking or a traveler. Create one shell per sellable unit when the arrangement is confirmed (SUP-12), starting with the information known from the block: category, maximum occupancy, date or service, and availability state.

**INV-06 · Agreed.** Once a supplier reservation exists for a shell, the shell carries its unit-level supplier confirmation and the supplier's physical assignment, such as a cabin or room number. The group contract reference stays on the arrangement (SUP-06).

**INV-38 · Agreed.** A shell's status is derived from its allocations and adjustments, never stored: Available, Partly allocated, Fully allocated or Returned to supplier. It is shown on screen and in reports.

**INV-07 · Agreed.** One shell may be allocated to several client bookings when the supplier allows shared occupancy. Traveler positions consume the shell's capacity. Each booking's travelers, charges and allocations stay separate.

**INV-08 · Agreed.** Recording a supplier's physical assignment identifies the resource; it never consumes additional capacity.

**INV-09 · Agreed.** Validate combined occupancy against simple, explicit supplier rules such as maximum occupancy and required adult count. Complex conditional occupancy rules are deferred.

### **Capacity pools**

**INV-10 · Agreed.** A capacity pool tracks interchangeable places as a quantity. Its remaining capacity is the opening quantity plus or minus dated adjustments, less active allocations.

**INV-11 · Agreed.** Ticket numbers, seat numbers and similar identifiers issued later are recorded as assignment details on the traveler or booking. Switch to shells only when the agency or supplier needs to track those resources individually.

### **Service category defaults**

**INV-12 · Agreed.** Each service category suggests a default representation:

| Service category | Default | Rules to retain |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hotel | Unit shells | Room shells for fixed-date package stays; night-by-night room capacity for variable stays (INV-13). Extra nights are a separate group when their terms or availability differ. |
| All-inclusive resort | Unit shells | Core stay as room-category shells with occupancy positions. Optional pre- and post-stays are linked extensions in separate groups when dates or availability differ. |
| Cruise | Unit shells | Cabin category, sailing and occupancy positions; physical cabin assignment may come later. |
| Air | Capacity pool | Capacity per flight segment (INV-14). |
| Rail | Seat pool, or shell for a private car | Capacity per segment (INV-15). |
| Transportation | Vehicle shell with a linked seat pool | INV-16. |
| Activity | Capacity pool | By activity, date, time and session. One place per traveler; adult, child and option choices do not split availability unless the supplier sets separate limits. |
| Meal | Capacity pool | By date or session. Meal choices are recorded separately and do not split availability unless the supplier sets separate limits. |
| Insurance | No shared inventory | Coverage is per household by default, or per traveler when the policy requires it. Track enrollment capacity only when the insurer sets a limit. |

**INV-13 · Agreed.** For a variable hotel stay, allocate room capacity night by night, using one room on each night of the stay.

**INV-14 · Agreed.** Group air capacity is tracked per flight segment, by travel date, origin and cabin class, for each air offering. A full-itinerary option uses one place per traveler on every included segment. A partial-itinerary option is a round trip that joins or leaves the group itinerary at a connecting point; it uses capacity only on its included segments. Outbound-only and return-only options are deferred; a traveler going one way only uses own-arranged travel (see Bookings and packages).

**INV-15 · Agreed.** Rail capacity is tracked per segment, by date, origin and service class, for each rail offering. Full- and partial-itinerary options use capacity as in INV-14. A private car uses one shell with passenger capacity.

**INV-16 · Agreed.** Each contracted vehicle is a shell, and its passenger places are a linked seat pool. When the agency commits identifiable seats, those seats are shells instead. Allocating the whole vehicle uses the vehicle shell, and the same passenger capacity is never counted twice.

### **Inventory allocations**

**INV-17 · Agreed.** An inventory allocation links one client booking to a shell, with its traveler positions, or to a quantity from a pool. A client booking may have allocations in several shells and pools; the services in a package keep separate allocations linked to the same booking.

**INV-18 · Agreed.** An allocation is Held, Reserved or Released, with the transitions in the allocation lifecycle table. Held and Reserved both consume capacity.

**INV-19 · Agreed.** Capacity conflict and Needs resolution are flags on an allocation, not allocation states. They are conditions (OPS-06): calculated from the records and cleared automatically when resolved. A flagged allocation keeps its state until staff resolve it.

**INV-20 · Agreed.** Only the explicit Hold space action creates an allocation. Saving or editing a draft client booking does not reserve space.

**INV-21 · Agreed.** Reserved means the agency's continuing allocation for the client. It does not mean the supplier has accepted an individual reservation.

**INV-22 · Agreed.** A hold against Courtesy or option space is conditional. Show the supplier's cutoff, and require staff to follow up or reconfirm before it.

**INV-23 · Agreed.** When a group has no remaining capacity, block new holds and any confirmation that needs additional capacity. An existing hold may be confirmed because its capacity is already allocated, except as limited by INV-28.

**INV-24 · Agreed.** Releasing a client's allocation returns the space to the agency's available inventory. It does not reduce the supplier commitment; returning space to the supplier is a separate adjustment (INV-26).

**INV-25 · Deferred.** Waitlists and supplier-approved overselling.

### **Inventory history and adjustments**

**INV-26 · Agreed.** Every change to inventory is a dated event: opening quantity, hold, allocation, release, supplier request and acceptance, assignment, cancellation, supplier release, adjustment and correction. Increases add shells or pool quantity. Reductions and releases initiated by the agency may use only unallocated capacity, and a reduction below active allocations is blocked.

**INV-27 · Agreed.** A supplier-driven reduction below active allocations is recorded, not blocked, and creates a capacity conflict: flag the affected allocations and their bookings, and pause new allocations from the group until staff resolve the shortfall. Unaffected bookings may proceed.

**INV-28 · Agreed.** While a capacity conflict is open, an affected hold stays capacity-consuming, is marked at risk and keeps its expiry, but cannot be confirmed. Affected allocations are never released or reassigned automatically.

**INV-29 · Agreed.** Staff resolve each affected booking individually, by same-category reassignment (INV-31), replacement inventory with an amendment, or cancellation. Automated prioritization and bulk resolution are deferred.

**INV-30 · Agreed.** When a supplier-driven reduction removes shells, staff choose which shells are removed, unallocated shells first, or the specific units the supplier's notice names. The record shows which allocations each removed shell affects.

**INV-31 · Agreed.** Staff may move a booking to another unit in the same category without client approval only when dates, occupancy, accepted price and all other client terms are unchanged. Record the reassignment and the supplier confirmation separately.

**INV-32 · Agreed.** Descriptive edits to inventory setup, such as labels and notes, are made in place with an audit entry. Once allocations exist, in-place changes to dates, category, occupancy limits, unit identity or structure, including converting between shells and a pool, are blocked. Staff create replacement inventory and resolve affected allocations explicitly, preserving the original setup and allocation history.

### **Supplier reservations**

**INV-33 · Agreed.** A supplier reservation record is created only when staff explicitly start a supplier request, at the scope the arrangement defines (SUP-10). An allocation alone never creates one. Creating the record is local tracking; it does not mean anything was sent to or accepted by the supplier.

**INV-34 · Agreed.** A supplier reservation is Requested, Confirmed, Declined or Cancelled, with the transitions in the supplier reservation lifecycle table. A confirmed group block may need no separate request for each allocation.

**INV-35 · Agreed.** The supplier's response and reference are recorded on the supplier reservation. Supplier acceptance never changes the client's accepted price; only an explicit amendment does.

**INV-36 · Agreed.** If a supplier declines, keep the client's acceptance, flag the service for staff action, and treat it as unfulfilled until staff arrange an accepted alternative, amend the client booking or cancel it.

**INV-37 · Agreed.** A client-side hold on an on-request or directly booked service never implies that the supplier is holding space. While the supplier reservation is Requested and the client booking is confirmed, the service shows as Awaiting supplier.

# **Clients and travelers**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. **Proposed** rules are new and need a decision. Rule prefix: **CLT**.

## **Context**

DepartureDesk keeps two kinds of profile. A **client** is a person or organization responsible for paying: it owes balances and receives invoices and statements. A **traveler** is a person who takes part in a trip: their date of birth, passport and emergency contacts live on the traveler profile. Keeping the two apart means financial history and personal travel data never mix, and it fits the many cases where the payer is not the traveler, such as a school paying for its students or a parent paying for an adult child.

A traveler may be linked to one or more clients, for example as themselves, a family member, or a member of an organization. A link records the relationship and makes booking faster; it grants no access and is never required. When the same person is both a client and a traveler, staff create both profiles together, linked as Self.

Client money (charges, payments, receipts and statements) is defined in Client money. Pricing by traveler type is defined in Bookings and packages. Documents, waivers and communications are defined in Travelers, documents, and communications. Audit logging of passport access is ACC-27.

## **Rules**

### **Client profiles**

**CLT-01 · Agreed.** A client profile represents a person or an organization responsible for paying. It records the type (person or organization), the name, contact points, a billing address and notes.

**CLT-02 · Agreed.** Each Client Trip has exactly one responsible client. The responsible client owes the trip's balance, and invoices and statements are addressed to it.

**CLT-03 · Agreed.** Every payer is a client profile. Any client profile may pay toward a Client Trip's charges without becoming its responsible client. The payer is recorded on each payment (see Client money).

**CLT-04 · Agreed.** Staff can quick-create a client profile, with a name and one contact point, while recording a payment from a payer who has no profile yet.

### **Traveler profiles**

**CLT-05 · Agreed.** A traveler profile always represents one individual person. Organizations are never travelers.

**CLT-06 · Agreed.** A traveler profile is reusable across trips and records the traveler's legal name, date of birth, supplier-required gender or sex, contact details, emergency contacts, and passport details (CLT-17 to CLT-20).

**CLT-07 · Agreed.** Dietary and accessibility needs are recorded on the specific service or booking where they matter, not as general traveler-profile fields.

**CLT-08 · Proposed.** Age is worked out from the traveler's date of birth as of the relevant service or Departure date, never stored. In MVP, staff still choose each traveler's type manually for pricing (see Bookings and packages); age is shown to help them.

### **Links between clients and travelers**

**CLT-09 · Agreed.** A traveler may be linked to any number of clients, including none. Each link has a relationship: Self, Family member, Employee or member, or Other.

**CLT-10 · Agreed.** A link is a convenience, not a permission. It lets staff choose a client's usual travelers when booking. It never gives the client, or anyone acting for the client, access to the traveler's personal data, and a traveler can be booked on a Client Trip without any link to its client.

**CLT-11 · Agreed.** When creating a client who is a person, staff may mark "this client is also traveling". This creates a traveler profile with the same name and contact details, linked as Self.

**CLT-12 · Agreed.** Profiles linked as Self are never synchronized silently. When staff edit a shared field (name, email, phone, address) on one, the system offers to make the same change on the other.

### **Travelers on Client Trips**

**CLT-13 · Agreed.** A traveler is placed on a client booking through a traveler assignment. The traveler's name as booked is snapshotted on the assignment, so later profile edits do not change confirmed records.

**CLT-14 · Proposed.** When staff submit traveler details to a supplier, the submitted values (such as name, date of birth and passport details) are snapshotted on the submission record, so the agency can show what it sent.

**CLT-15 · Agreed.** Each traveler detail is required at the stage shown in the traveler details table below. A hold may use placeholder travelers, such as "Guest 2", and confirmation is blocked until every placeholder is replaced by a traveler with a legal name and date of birth.

**CLT-16 · Proposed.** Before a supplier submission (names, rooming list, manifest or ticketing), the system shows a checklist of missing details and passports that expire before the trip ends. Country-specific passport validity rules are deferred.

### **Passport and sensitive data**

**CLT-17 · Agreed.** Passport details (number, issuing country, nationality, expiry date) are stored on the traveler profile and are required for any service that needs them. An image of the passport information page is optional. This supersedes the September plan to hold passport images in an external document repository.

**CLT-18 · Agreed.** Only staff with a specific permission can view or download passport details (see ACC-11). A reseller may enter and view passport details, and upload a passport image, only for travelers on bookings currently assigned to it; access ends when the booking is reassigned (ACC-14). Every view is logged (ACC-27).

**CLT-26 · Agreed.** In MVP, agency staff or the assigned reseller enter traveler details; travelers do not submit them through DepartureDesk. The agency's operating guidance must cover secure collection of passport details and deleting emailed or printed copies once entered. A secure traveler-details form that needs no login is deferred.

**CLT-19 · Agreed.** Share with a supplier only the traveler information that booking needs. Every staff view, download and supplier share of passport records is logged (ACC-27).

**CLT-20 · Agreed.** Staff with the passport permission may delete a traveler's passport details and image at any time, for example at the traveler's request. The deletion is audited, and booking and financial history stays intact.

**CLT-39 · Review.** Automatic retention periods for traveler and passport data, including copies submitted to suppliers, need the agency's legal adviser and will become a later agency setting.

### **Contacts**

**CLT-21 · Agreed.** Profiles record contact points of three kinds: email, phone and postal address. Each has a label, and one may be marked preferred for each kind. When an email sent by DepartureDesk bounces or is undeliverable, the address is marked undeliverable and staff are shown the failure. Marketing consent and contact verification are deferred.

**CLT-27 · Agreed.** An organization client may record named contact people as contact points with a name and a role, such as trip coordinator or accounts payable. They are not separate profiles in MVP.

**CLT-22 · Agreed.** A Client Trip records contacts by purpose: booking, billing, on-trip, emergency, parent or guardian, and group leader. Each points to a contact point on a client or traveler profile. A group leader is a contact purpose only, with no login (ACC-19).

**CLT-40 · Agreed.** When a traveler will be under 18 on the departure date, flag the booking if it has no adult traveler and no parent or guardian contact. The flag is a warning and blocks nothing; supplier rules such as a required adult per cabin are enforced by INV-09.

### **Duplicates and inactivation**

**CLT-23 · Proposed.** When staff create a profile, warn about likely duplicates: by name and email for clients, and by name and date of birth for travelers. Staff may select the existing profile or create a new one anyway, which is audited. Profiles are never merged automatically; merging is deferred.

**CLT-24 · Proposed.** A profile used on any booking, payment or document can be made inactive but never deleted. Inactive profiles cannot be added to new bookings, and historical records still show them.

### **Reseller visibility**

**CLT-25 · Proposed.** A reseller sees client and traveler details only as they appear on bookings currently assigned to it. It never sees a profile's other trips, links or history. A reseller may create client and traveler profiles while creating a booking.

### **Identity fields**

**CLT-28 · Agreed.** A traveler's legal name is recorded as given names, middle name, surname and suffix, as they appear on the passport, with an optional preferred name used in conversation and correspondence but never sent to suppliers.

**CLT-29 · Agreed.** Supplier-required gender or sex includes X for passports that use it.

**CLT-30 · Agreed.** A traveler profile holds a list of supplier identifiers, each a program and a number, such as frequent flyer numbers, cruise past-guest numbers, Known Traveler numbers and redress numbers.

### **Corrections and substitutions**

**CLT-31 · Agreed.** A correction to the same person's details is made on the traveler profile. It applies automatically to draft and held bookings. For confirmed bookings, the system lists those using the old details; staff choose which to update, and a confirmed booking's snapshot changes only when staff apply the correction to it.

**CLT-32 · Agreed.** Replacing one traveler with a different person is a substitution: an amendment that assigns another traveler profile to the same allocation and position. The original traveler's profile is never edited to become the new person. The amendment rechecks traveler type, occupancy and required-adult rules, and any price change needs the client's acceptance.

**CLT-33 · Agreed.** If the changed details were already submitted to a supplier, the correction or substitution creates a follow-up task for a new supplier submission. Staff record any supplier-confirmed fee as a supplier obligation. Client terms may include an optional change fee, as a pass-through of supplier fees, an agency fee, or both (defined in Bookings and packages); a fee the accepted terms do not cover needs the client's acceptance or is absorbed by the agency. Where the supplier refuses the change, staff use the cancellation and rebooking flow.

### **Responsible client changes and refunds**

**CLT-34 · Agreed.** Staff may change a Client Trip's responsible client as an explicit action with a recorded reason. Invoices, receipts and statements already issued keep their original addressee; only later documents use the new client.

**CLT-35 · Agreed.** A refund goes by default to the payer who made the payment, by the same method. Staff may direct it elsewhere only with a recorded reason. This rule moves to Client money when that area is drafted.

### **Traveler data on documents**

**CLT-36 · Agreed.** Client documents show travelers' names and their service details only. Dates of birth, passport details, supplier identifiers and emergency contacts never appear on client documents. Travelers on other bookings who share a unit appear by name only, if at all.

### **Import**

**CLT-37 · Agreed.** MVP includes a basic import of client and traveler profiles from a file. Bookings, payments and financial history are not imported.

**CLT-38 · Agreed.** An import uses a downloadable template file and shows a preview before saving. It flags likely duplicates, both within the file and against existing profiles, using the CLT-23 checks without merging them, and reports rows it cannot read. It can create Self links where the file marks a client as also a traveler; other relationships are added by hand. Passport details may be imported only by staff with the passport permission, passport images are not imported, and every import is audited.

# **Bookings and packages**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussions of 28 October. **Deferred** marks items outside MVP. Rule prefix: **BKG**.

## **Context**

A Client Trip holds everything one client is buying. Each purchase on it is a client booking: one package, one standalone service, or one trip-specific bundle, for a set of travelers. A booking starts as a draft, where staff or the assigned reseller can review its current price and terms and hold shared space when appropriate. They may share an optional client summary. The client accepts the current price and terms before confirmation; confirmation fixes the accepted price and terms. From then on, every change is an explicit amendment or cancellation, and nothing reprices silently.

Packages are client-facing offers. They group services without merging the supplier arrangements, inventory or costs behind them, and clients choose options within them: a cabin category, an air option, add-ons. Client terms set what happens when a client changes or cancels, through a cancellation schedule and a change-fee schedule that are fixed for each booking when it is confirmed.

Inventory allocations and supplier reservations are defined in Inventory; travelers, substitutions and corrections in Clients and travelers; charges, payments, installments and refunds in Client money; postponement in Departures; optional client summaries and confirmation documents in Documents and communications.

## **Rules**

### **Client Trips and components**

**BKG-01 · Agreed.** A Client Trip component is a service a traveler needs, such as a cruise, pre-night hotel, transfer, activity or insurance. It can exist before it is booked and needs no group inventory.

**BKG-02 · Agreed.** A component is fulfilled by a group allocation, a direct supplier reservation, an externally arranged service (recorded on the trip with no agency inventory), or is still planned. A component may have several fulfillments, such as two rooms for one hotel stay.

**BKG-03 · Agreed.** A Client Trip's status is derived from its bookings, never stored: Quote (at least one draft and nothing confirmed), Not booked (every booking discarded), Booked (at least one confirmed booking), Travelled (all services in the past) or Cancelled (every confirmed booking cancelled). Lead and inquiry tracking is deferred.

**BKG-04 · Agreed.** Staff may link a Client Trip with no Departure to a Departure later. Receipts already issued are unchanged; later receipts follow the per-Departure rule (see Client money).

### **Client bookings**

**BKG-05 · Agreed.** A client booking is one purchase of one offering (a package, a standalone service, or a trip-specific package under BKG-20) for a set of travelers on one Client Trip. It has one client charge and its own reference, shown with the Client Trip reference.

**BKG-06 · Agreed.** A client booking is Draft, Confirmed, Discarded or Cancelled, with the transitions in the client booking lifecycle table. Discarded ends a booking that was never confirmed; Cancelled ends a confirmed one and always records an initiator (DEP-22). A booking's status is independent of its inventory allocations (INV-18) and supplier reservations (INV-34); no status implies another.

**BKG-07 · Agreed.** One Client Trip may hold bookings under different accepted terms, such as package terms and a separate hotel's terms. The client sees one consolidated trip balance (see Client money).

**BKG-08 · Agreed.** After confirmation, adding a service the booking's offering includes as an add-on is an amendment to that booking. Adding any other service creates a new booking on the same Client Trip.

### **Holds**

**BKG-09 · Agreed.** A hold lasts for the agency's hold duration, 2 calendar days by default, which a Departure may override. Its expiry is 11:59 p.m. agency local time on the last day. Staff may extend a hold once, recording the new expiry; extending past a supplier cutoff requires staff to reconfirm availability.

**BKG-10 · Agreed.** When a hold expires, its allocations are released (INV-24) and the booking owner is notified. The booking stays Draft, and staff or the reseller may place a new hold if space remains.

**BKG-11 · Agreed.** A package-level Hold space action checks every inventory-linked service in the package first, and creates holds for all of them only if each can be held. If any cannot, it creates none and names the unavailable services. On-request and directly booked services follow their own lifecycle and do not block the package hold. Staff may still hold individual services.

**BKG-12 · Agreed.** Holds apply only to shared inventory. For a standalone service, a supplier's option or courtesy hold is tracked as a deadline on the supplier reservation (SUP-22).

### **Offerings, choices and add-ons**

**BKG-13 · Agreed.** A package is a client-facing offer that groups Client Trip components. It never merges their supplier arrangements, inventory, supplier reservations or costs.

**BKG-14 · Agreed.** Each choice in an offering is made per traveler, per unit or per Client Trip, and follows one of three patterns: required single (exactly one option), optional single (zero or one), or optional multiple (any number of additive options). Optional multiple applies only to add-ons. Required choices may be incomplete on a draft but must be complete to confirm.

**BKG-15 · Agreed.** Changing an included service, such as the cabin category, means selecting another option in its choice. An add-on only adds a service.

**BKG-16 · Agreed.** Add-ons are itemized beneath the package price. An add-on may be chosen for only some travelers; only those travelers use its capacity and pay its price, and they stay on the same booking as the others.

**BKG-17 · Agreed.** Before confirmation, staff or the reseller handling the booking may change which travelers take an add-on, with availability rechecked. After confirmation, add-on changes are amendments; a reseller may request one, and staff check capacity and supplier effects.

**BKG-18 · Agreed.** Where a service is part of an offering, an own-arranged option may be offered. It creates no supplier reservation or inventory use. Its price is zero or a stated credit entered as a separate client-price adjustment, never a negative supplier cost. The traveler's arrival and departure details are recorded when manifests or transfers need them.

**BKG-19 · Agreed.** Group air is sold as a round trip, or as a partial itinerary under INV-14. A traveler going one way only uses own-arranged travel.

**BKG-20 · Agreed.** On a Client Trip with no Departure, staff may group several standalone services into a trip-specific package: one booking with one staff-entered price. Each service keeps its own supplier reservation and cost. A trip-specific package has no choices, holds or shared inventory.

### **Pricing**

**BKG-21 · Agreed.** Staff set offering prices for each traveler type, occupancy position, supplier-stated traveler-specific fare and choice option. When booking, staff or the reseller choose each traveler's type manually; age is shown (CLT-08) but never chooses the type.

**BKG-22 · Agreed.** A booking without an offering is priced directly by staff, as a total or per traveler.

**BKG-23 · Agreed.** Changing an offering's prices never changes an existing booking.

**BKG-24 · Agreed. A draft or held booking keeps its prices until staff or the reseller handling it refresh them. When the offering's prices change, the booking shows a "prices changed" indicator. A refresh updates prices and the client terms version together, shows the old and new price per traveler and the total difference before saving, and marks any client summary already shared as out of date. Acceptance of the earlier price or terms does not cover the refreshed version.**

**BKG-25 · Agreed.** Confirmation creates the client charge from a snapshot of each traveler's accepted price, traveler type, occupancy position and chosen options, together with the accepted terms. A hold creates no charge.

**BKG-26 · Agreed.** A confirmed booking is never repriced automatically. Staff may lower its price through an amendment with a reason, which needs no client acceptance; any increase needs acceptance. This supersedes the Ideal Model's deferral of repricing confirmed bookings.

**BKG-27 · Deferred.** Choosing traveler type from age, promotions, combinations of pricing rules, automated repricing, and price suggestions.

### **Client terms**

**BKG-28 · Agreed.** Staff finalize client terms for a Departure or package from the agency's reusable terms plus Departure- or package-specific terms. The accepted version is snapshotted on the booking at confirmation.

**BKG-29 · Agreed. For a booking with no Departure, staff start from the agency's default terms and may edit them for that booking before presenting its price and terms to the client.**

**BKG-30 · Agreed.** Any client-facing payment fee is disclosed in the terms before confirmation, and applies later only as the accepted terms authorize (see Client money).

**BKG-31 · Agreed.** The client cancellation schedule is an agency default with an optional Departure override, snapshotted at confirmation. Basic date-and-percentage tiers calculate a proposed charge from the affected service's accepted client charge, for staff to review before recording. Schedules outside that structure are entered manually. Client and supplier cancellation schedules are independent, and changing either never changes confirmed terms.

**BKG-32 · Agreed.** The client change-fee schedule is an agency default with an optional Departure override, snapshotted at confirmation. For each change type (name correction, substitution, component replacement, transfer), it states an agency fee as a fixed amount per traveler or per booking, or none; whether supplier fees pass through; and an optional free-until date.

**BKG-33 · Agreed.** Change fees apply only to changes the client requests. Changes made by a supplier or decided by the agency, such as a postponement, never carry a client change fee.

**BKG-34 · Agreed.** Removing a traveler or an add-on is a partial cancellation under the cancellation schedule, not a change. Adding a traveler or add-on carries no change fee.

**BKG-35 · Agreed.** When a supplier fee passes through, staff enter the client charge explicitly, since currencies may differ. Staff may waive or reduce any change fee with a recorded reason.

**BKG-36 · Agreed.** A change fee authorized by the accepted terms needs no new acceptance when the change does not otherwise alter the price.

### **Client summaries and acceptance**

**BKG-37 · Agreed. Staff or the reseller handling a booking may share an optional client summary from a draft. It shows current prices, chosen options, the payment schedule, client terms, whether space is held ("held until" a date, or "subject to availability"), and an optional "price valid until" date. A separate proposal step or document is not required for acceptance or confirmation. A summary contains no sensitive traveler data (CLT-36). Document mechanics are in Documents and communications.**

**BKG-38 · Agreed. An optional client summary may present several draft bookings on the same Client Trip as alternatives. When the client accepts one, staff confirm that booking and discard the others, releasing their holds.**

**BKG-39 · Agreed. Confirmation requires recorded client acceptance of the booking's current price and terms. A separate proposal is not required. If those details change after acceptance, the earlier acceptance no longer covers the changed booking; any summary already shared is marked out of date, and confirmation waits for acceptance of the updated price and terms.**

**BKG-40 · Agreed. If an optional "price valid until" date passes before the client accepts the current price and terms, show a warning; staff decide whether to honor the price or refresh it.**

**BKG-41 · Agreed. An acceptance record captures who accepted (the responsible client, or the named person acting for an organization), the date, method, price and terms accepted, and who recorded it. A designated deposit or payment counts as acceptance only when the client terms explicitly say so; staff record or verify that payment and link the acceptance to its separate financial record. A different payment does not establish acceptance. For email or signed acceptance, retain the evidence; for phone or in-person acceptance, a staff note is enough. Confirmation records acceptance in the booking flow; no separate acceptance form is required.**

**BKG-42 · Agreed.** The same acceptance record is used for amendments that change price or terms, substitutions, transfers and postponement offers, pointing to the amendment or offer instead of a proposal.

**BKG-43 · Deferred.** A click-to-accept link that needs no login, and certified electronic signatures.

**BKG-44 · Agreed.** Staff may discard a draft at any time, which makes it Discarded and releases its holds, with no charge, refund or initiator. Drafts are never deleted or discarded automatically, and lists can filter by Client Trip status and age.

### **Confirmation**

**BKG-45 · Agreed. Staff, or the reseller the booking is assigned to (ACC-15), may confirm a booking when the client's acceptance of its current price and terms is recorded, every required choice is complete, every traveler has a legal name and date of birth (CLT-15), and capacity is available with no open capacity conflict (INV-23, INV-28). Confirmation turns the booking's held allocations into Reserved.**

**BKG-46 · Agreed. Confirmation does not require payment. The deposit is the first installment. An overdue deposit flags the booking in Needs attention as space at risk and creates follow-up in the single staff task queue; staff may cancel the booking under its accepted terms, and it is never cancelled automatically.**

### **Amendments**

**BKG-47 · Agreed.** Every change to a confirmed booking is an explicit amendment. Original records are preserved, and changes are recorded as adjustments rather than overwrites.

**BKG-48 · Agreed.** In a confirmed package, staff may replace one service through a component-level amendment that keeps the booking, the Client Trip and the unaffected services. The original service's release or cancellation and the replacement's reservation or allocation are linked records, with supplier outcomes and costs kept separate. Price and payment changes are linked charge, credit or refund adjustments; earlier payments are never moved automatically. Changed price or terms need the client's acceptance. A change that cannot be expressed as replacing one service is a cancellation and rebooking.

**BKG-49 · Agreed.** A traveler may be transferred between bookings only when both are on the same Departure and for the same offering, the traveler's chosen options are available on the destination booking, and the supplier permits it. Any other move is a component replacement or a cancellation and rebooking.

**BKG-50 · Agreed.** A transfer is one coordinated amendment to both bookings. The traveler stays in the same unit, shared across the two bookings (INV-07), or moves to a unit on the destination booking after a capacity check. The traveler keeps their accepted price unless their occupancy position changes; a decrease needs no acceptance and an increase does. The change fee follows the source booking's terms, and a follow-up task is created for a new supplier submission.

**BKG-51 · Agreed.** A transfer between bookings on different Client Trips needs acceptance from both responsible clients. Within one Client Trip, one acceptance is enough.

**BKG-52 · Agreed.** In a transfer, payments stay where they are. Staff record a credit on the source booking and a charge on the destination booking; balances never move automatically. Applying a credit across Client Trips needs the source responsible client's recorded consent (rule to be placed in Client money).

**BKG-53 · Agreed.** Moving a booking to another Departure is a cancellation and rebooking, except an accepted postponement to a linked Departure (see Departures). A general move action is deferred.

Substitutions and corrections of travelers are defined in CLT-31 to CLT-33.

### **Cancellation**

**BKG-54 · Agreed.** Staff cancel through one Complete cancellation flow, which cancels the booking or the chosen travelers and releases their allocations. It shows supplier cancellation or release only when relevant, and client credit or refund details only when money is involved. Pending supplier or financial outcomes stay visible but never block the client cancellation, and supplier and client effects are recorded separately.

**BKG-55 · Agreed.** Cancelling some of a booking's travelers is an amendment: the cancellation schedule applies to those travelers' portion, and their traveler assignments are marked cancelled. Cancelling every traveler cancels the booking.

**BKG-56 · Agreed.** A reseller may discard an unconfirmed booking assigned to it while its hold is active, releasing the allocations immediately with no charge or refund. For a confirmed booking, a reseller's cancellation request does not cancel anything; staff complete the cancellation.

**BKG-57 · Agreed.** Staff enter client refund and credit amounts under the client's accepted terms; they are never derived automatically from a supplier refund or penalty (see Client money).

### **Shared-unit cancellation**

**BKG-58 · Agreed.** When a traveler in a shared unit cancels, the remaining traveler's booking and accepted price stay unchanged, and the vacant position is marked potentially available. Staff may seek a replacement within a recovery window. The system proposes its cutoff, which staff may change: a set number of days (7 by default, as an agency setting) before the earlier of the relevant supplier's names deadline and final payment date, and never later than either.

**BKG-59 · Agreed.** A replacement is confirmed only after staff verify the supplier permits the shared occupancy. It is a separate booking sharing the same unit, with its own charges and payments.

**BKG-60 · Agreed.** If no replacement is confirmed by the cutoff, staff offer the remaining traveler an occupancy-based price through an amendment, optionally reduced by an agency-funded discount, or the agency absorbs the difference. If the traveler declines or does not respond, the booking and price stay unchanged and the uncovered supplier cost is flagged for staff. Nothing is charged, cancelled or released without explicit acceptance or staff action.

**BKG-61 · Agreed.** The departing traveler's cancellation charges, credits and refund are recorded on that traveler's own booking.

# **Departures**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. Rule prefix: **DEP**.

## **Context**

A Departure is the dated group product the agency plans and sells. Three things about it move independently, so each has its own status. The sales status says whether the agency has chosen to offer it. The operating decision says whether the agency intends to run it, postpone it or cancel it. Completion says whether it has finished: travelled, cancelled or superseded by a postponement, and then financially reconciled. Staff see one summary label derived from the three.

The system never decides for the agency. It gathers what staff need for each decision, flags changes that may affect commitments already made, and records who decided what and when. When a Departure is cancelled or postponed, each client booking is resolved individually, and nothing is cancelled, moved or repriced automatically.

Client bookings, holds, client terms and amendments are defined in Bookings and packages; inventory and capacity conflicts in Inventory; supplier arrangements in Suppliers and arrangements; profitability, cancellation exposure and closeout money in Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout; task mechanics and queues in Operations.

## **Rules**

### **The Departure record**

**DEP-01 · Agreed.** A Departure records its name, description and itinerary, travel start and end dates, one client-facing currency (see Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout), the default time zone for its services (SET-10) and its Departure team (ACC-09). It may override the agency's hold duration (BKG-09), cancellation schedule (BKG-31), change-fee schedule (BKG-32), card fee (see Client money) and client terms (BKG-28).

**DEP-02 · Deferred.** A reusable travel program or series grouping related Departures.

### **Statuses**

**DEP-03 · Agreed.** A Departure has three independent statuses: a sales status, an operating decision and a completion status, each with the transitions in its table below.

**DEP-04 · Agreed.** Staff see one summary label derived from the three statuses, as in the summary label table. The label is never stored.

**DEP-05 · Agreed.** New holds and bookings are allowed only while the Departure is On sale: Ready to sell, with an operating decision of Undecided or Proceed, and completion Active.

### **Readiness and publication**

**DEP-06 · Agreed.** Staff with the Departure-management permission set a Departure to Ready to sell or return it to Draft. MVP calculates no viability and blocks nothing automatically.

**DEP-07 · Agreed.** Only a Departure that is Ready to sell can be published. Staff publish offers explicitly to each reseller channel, and resellers see only offers published to their channel (ACC-14).

**DEP-08 · Agreed.** Returning a Departure to Draft withdraws its offers from every reseller channel and prevents new holds. Confirmed bookings and existing holds keep their state, expiry and recorded terms; staff resolve any affected hold through the normal workflow. Publishing again requires Ready to sell and an explicit publish action.

**DEP-09 · Agreed.** Offers stay published while review items are open. Staff may unpublish manually.

**DEP-10 · Agreed.** A reseller cannot confirm a booking whose prices or terms differ from the current offering; it must refresh the booking first (BKG-24). Only staff may confirm a booking at superseded prices or terms.

**DEP-11 · Agreed.** When a published offer changes materially, or a Postpone or Cancel decision pauses sales, reseller users on each affected channel receive an in-app notice describing the change. Email notices are deferred.

### **Review items**

**DEP-12 · Agreed.** A review item is created when either of the following happens:

- On a Departure that is Ready to sell, any change to its dates, included services or choices, prices, client terms, supplier capacity or commitments, or key deadlines.  
- At any time, a supplier deadline change that affects client due dates on existing bookings, or a change to which supplier terms apply (SUP-19).

Other edits before Ready to sell create no review item.

**DEP-13 · Agreed.** A review item records what changed, with old and new values, who changed it and when, and an impact list of the records that may need attention: holds, draft bookings whose proposals are now out of date, confirmed bookings whose due dates or terms may be affected, and published offers. The Departure shows how many review items are open, and they appear in the Needs attention queue.

**DEP-14 · Agreed.** Staff with the Departure-management permission clear a review item with a note, and clearing is logged. Clearing records that the impact was considered and changes nothing; any follow-up happens through the normal workflows.

**DEP-15 · Agreed.** Review items block nothing and change no status.

### **Go/no-go**

**DEP-16 · Agreed.** The operating decision is made manually by staff with the Departure-management permission, separately from Ready to sell. The system calculates no outcome.

**DEP-17 · Agreed.** A Departure may have a review date. The Departure shows its earliest money-committing supplier deadline, such as a release date, minimum deadline or final payment, as a reference. Setting a review date creates a follow-up task for the decision owner (see Operations). After a Proceed decision, staff may set another review date.

**DEP-18 · Agreed.** The decision panel shows, without calculating a verdict: confirmed travelers and units against each supplier minimum (SUP-26), space currently held, committed cost exposure (SUP-13), upcoming supplier deadlines, the cancellation-exposure roll-up and projected margin (see Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout).

**DEP-19 · Agreed.** Each decision records its value, who made it and when. A note is required for Postpone and Cancel and optional for Proceed. Every earlier decision stays in the history.

**DEP-20 · Agreed.** A decision may be changed with a note. Changing a decision undoes nothing already done; for example, bookings cancelled under a Cancel decision stay cancelled if the decision becomes Proceed.

**DEP-21 · Agreed.** Proceed records intent only. Postpone pauses new sales; Cancel stops them. Neither cancels client bookings, releases supplier commitments or creates refunds by itself.

### **Departure cancellation**

**DEP-22 · Agreed.** Every client booking cancellation records its initiator: client-requested, agency-initiated or supplier-initiated.

**DEP-23 · Agreed.** Client terms include an "if we cancel" clause, defaulting to a full refund of amounts paid. It applies to agency-initiated cancellations in place of the client's cancellation schedule, and no change fees apply. Staff enter the actual amounts (BKG-57). Staff may offer a travel credit instead only with the client's acceptance; using the credit on another Client Trip follows BKG-52.

**DEP-24 · Agreed.** After a Cancel decision, staff may release every hold and discard every draft booking on the Departure in one action. Confirmed bookings are cancelled one by one through the Complete cancellation flow (BKG-54); there is no bulk cancellation of confirmed bookings.

**DEP-25 · Agreed.** A cancellation worklist shows every confirmed booking with its status and overall progress, and every supplier arrangement still needing a cancellation, deposit disposition or benefit closeout. A booking is resolved once it is cancelled.

**DEP-26 · Agreed.** Completion moves to Cancelled automatically once, under a Cancel decision, every booking is resolved and every supplier arrangement is Closed or Cancelled.

### **Postponement**

**DEP-27 · Agreed.** A Postpone decision may come before new dates are known. Creating the linked Departure is a separate staff action, taken once the new dates are settled. The original Departure keeps its dates and history.

**DEP-28 · Agreed.** The linked Departure copies, as starting values: the description and itinerary, offerings and their choices, prices, client terms as a new draft version, the Departure team, overrides for hold duration and cancellation and change fees, the time zone and the currency. It does not copy supplier arrangements, inventory, bookings, holds, publication, review items or the operating decision. It starts as Draft, Undecided and Active.

**DEP-29 · Agreed.** When a supplier moves its group contract and deposits to the new dates, the contract becomes a new arrangement on the linked Departure, and the deposits move as a supplier credit applied across arrangements under the same payee account (see Supplier money).

**DEP-30 · Agreed.** For each confirmed booking, staff record a postponement offer: the revised dates, price and terms offered, the intended recipient, the delivery date and status, the response deadline and the response. DepartureDesk does not send offers, and recording an offer or response changes nothing on the booking. A materially revised offer is recorded as a new version, keeping the earlier offer and response history.

**DEP-31 · Agreed.** The response deadline defaults to 14 calendar days after the recorded delivery, with a per-offer override; staff may shorten it when a supplier or operational deadline requires. Staff are reminded 7 and 2 calendar days before the deadline; these reminders are internal and never sent to clients. An offer past its deadline is flagged overdue for staff follow-up, and nothing happens automatically.

**DEP-32 · Agreed.** Staff record the client's acceptance (BKG-42) before amending the booking. The amendment links the Client Trip to the new Departure, records a credit against the original charge and a new charge under the new Departure at the offered price, and applies the credit to the new charge; no extra consent is needed, since both are on the same Client Trip. Payments already receipted under the original Departure stay as they are, and later receipts follow the new Departure.

**DEP-33 · Agreed.** Within one booking, some travelers may accept and others decline. The amendment moves the travelers who accept and cancels those who decline. Other bookings on the same Client Trip are handled individually.

**DEP-34 · Agreed.** A client who declines is cancelled through the regular cancellation flow, recorded as agency-initiated (DEP-23).

**DEP-35 · Agreed.** A booking with no response stays unchanged pending staff action. If the original supplier space is withdrawn while an offer is unanswered, the booking and its accepted price and terms stay unchanged; the supplier reservation or availability shows as withdrawn; the booking is flagged Needs resolution in the Needs attention queue; its allocation is kept, marked at risk; and new allocations against the affected inventory pause. Nothing is moved, repriced, cancelled or released automatically, and staff may shorten the response period.

**DEP-36 · Agreed.** The original Departure's completion moves to Superseded automatically once every booking on it has moved or been cancelled. It still needs to be financially reconciled.

### **Changing Departure dates**

**DEP-37 · Agreed.** Before any booking is confirmed, staff may change a Departure's dates freely. Inventory with holds stays protected by INV-32, so staff release the holds or create replacement inventory. On a Departure that is Ready to sell, the change creates a review item.

**DEP-38 · Agreed.** Once bookings are confirmed, staff change the schedule by one of two routes, chosen with a recorded reason: a minor schedule change (DEP-39) or a postponement.

**DEP-39 · Agreed.** In a minor schedule change, staff update the affected services through replacement inventory or supplier changes and adjust the Departure's dates. A review item lists every affected booking, clients are informed through reissued trip documents, and client acceptance is needed only if price or terms change.

**DEP-40 · Agreed.** Client terms may include an optional schedule-change clause stating which changes the agency may make without the client's agreement. It is snapshotted with the terms. When the clause does not cover a change, staff use postponement.

**DEP-41 · Agreed.** Moving one service to another day within the Departure's dates is a change to that service (see Inventory and Bookings and packages), not a change to the Departure's dates.

### **Completion**

**DEP-42 · Agreed.** Staff mark a Departure Departed. The system prompts them on the day after the last service date.

**DEP-43 · Agreed. Once a Departure is Departed, no new bookings or holds are allowed, and amendments are limited to post-travel adjustments such as credits, refunds and corrections. It remains operationally Departed while financial follow-up is open. Open items stay visible in the staff task queue and on the Departure.**

**DEP-44 · Agreed. The Departure lists open financial items: client balances and refunds due, unapplied client credits, supplier amounts due, supplier-held and agency-held funds, commission and supplier-refund receivables, unallocated shared costs, benefits without a closeout status, and confirmed supplier credits. Each item is marked as a closeout blocker or follow-up. Client balances/refunds due, unapplied client funds, supplier amounts due, unmatched held funds, unallocated costs, benefits without disposition, and unresolved supplier decisions block reconciliation until settled or explicitly adjusted. A confirmed, quantified and attributed unpaid commission or supplier refund may remain as a linked supplier receivable in the staff follow-up queue; it does not block reconciliation and is excluded from actual margin until received or offset. A confirmed unused supplier credit with a recorded disposition does not block reconciliation (SMN-46).**

**DEP-45 · Agreed. Staff may reopen financial reconciliation with a reason when a late commission correction, supplier refund or other material adjustment arrives. Reopening is logged; the operational completion status remains unchanged (normally Departed). The previous reconciled margin snapshot stays available while staff review the adjustment. Reconciliation publishes a revised snapshot and retains the prior one.**

**DEP-46 · Agreed. Closeout means the Departure is Financially reconciled, with no blocking open items. Profitability is projected until the first closeout snapshot, then actual based on settled and otherwise final facts; confirmed unpaid supplier receivables stay visible and do not count in actual margin until received or offset. Reopening keeps the prior actual snapshot visible with an adjustment in progress; reconciliation publishes a revised actual snapshot. Supplier arrangements close at closeout (SUP-11).**

# **Supplier money**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. **Review** marks a rule that needs the agency's accountant. Rule prefix: **SMN**.

## **Context**

Supplier money runs in both directions. The agency owes suppliers for what it has committed to buy, and suppliers owe the agency commission, refunds and credits. Three kinds of record separate the questions staff ask most: cost lines say what a commitment will cost in total, schedule lines say when portions fall due, and amounts due say what is owed today. Payments settle amounts due, never costs directly.

Supplier money is organized around what each supplier contracted for, such as a cabin, a coach or a per-person fee, not around client bookings. The two meet through inventory allocations and, when needed, reporting attribution. Supplier-direct client payments reduce the Client Trip receivable but are not agency cash; any funds the supplier holds for the agency remain a separate balance. In MVP, staff can record a supplier obligation and payment directly without first configuring cost schedules or the full supplier-account path. Advanced matching, unusual settlement cases and broad supplier-account reporting can follow later.

Arrangements, terms and deadlines are defined in Suppliers and arrangements; client charges, client payments, collection routes and refunds in Client money; margin, attribution, currency and closeout in Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout.

## **Rules**

### **Cost, schedule and amounts due**

**SMN-01 · Agreed.** Supplier money uses three kinds of record: cost lines (what the agency will pay in total), schedule lines (when portions fall due) and amounts due (what is payable now).

**SMN-02 · Agreed.** A cost line states its scope (arrangement, category or pool, unit shell, supplier reservation, client booking or traveler), its basis (a fixed amount for the scope, a fixed amount per unit, person or night, or a percentage of a named, known cost base) and whether it is estimated or committed. Committed lines make up committed cost exposure (SUP-13); estimated lines stay separate as forecasts.

**SMN-03 · Agreed.** A deposit is a payment toward the commitment, never a cost added to it.

**SMN-04 · Agreed.** A schedule line states an amount or basis and when it falls due: on a fixed date, when an event occurs, a stated period after an event, or at the earlier of an event and a date. Schedule lines may be recorded before the final cost is known.

**SMN-05 · Agreed. Staff may record a straightforward supplier obligation directly as an amount due, with supplier, amount and due date, without first configuring cost lines or a schedule. Otherwise, an amount due is posted when a schedule line's date arrives or its event is recorded. Only amounts due are payables. In MVP, open amounts are visible from the Departure; a separate Financial review queue and broad supplier-account view may be phased later.**

**SMN-06 · Agreed.** A percentage or count-based amount is calculated and snapshotted when the amount due is posted. A later change to the base or count is an explicit adjustment, never a recalculation.

**SMN-07 · Agreed.** A supplier amendment adjusts the affected cost lines and current obligations while preserving the original records. Past due events and settlements are never rewritten, and the remaining amount is recalculated from the amended terms.

**SMN-08 · Agreed.** A payable stays at the supplier's scope, such as per cabin, whichever client bookings use it. A schedule line's scope need not match the scope used to attribute cost for reporting.

**SMN-09 · Agreed.** A client charge owed to a supplier creates no cost line or payable for the agency. The agency's income from it is the supplier commission.

**SMN-10 · Agreed.** Each supplier obligation and receivable links to the source that explains it: the arrangement, a unit shell, a client booking or a shared group cost. A per-unit deposit links to its unit shell even before a client booking is assigned. A supplier reservation or client booking may also be linked for traceability without changing the source.

### **Events that make an amount due**

**SMN-11 · Agreed.** A schedule line can be triggered only by an event from this list:

| Trigger event | Recorded when |
| :---- | :---- |
| Arrangement confirmed | Staff confirm the arrangement (SUP-11) |
| Names submitted | Staff record a names submission (CLT-14) |
| Rooming list submitted | Staff record a rooming-list submission |
| Final count or manifest submitted | Staff record the submission and supplier outcome |
| Tickets issued | Staff record ticketing |
| Supplier invoice received | Staff record the supplier's invoice and reference |
| Service date | The first service date under the arrangement arrives |
| Other | Staff record the event manually, with a date and reference |

**SMN-12 · Agreed.** Events are recorded by the normal action listed, and completing the matching follow-up task (SUP-22) records the same event, so no event is entered twice.

**SMN-13 · Agreed.** The event date is the date the event actually happened, which may be earlier than when it is recorded, and any waiting period runs from it. Under "earlier of an event and a date", the date still posts the amount due if the event never happens.

**SMN-14 · Agreed.** An amount that depends on a count is calculated from the count recorded with the event and snapshotted.

**SMN-15 · Agreed.** Events are never undone automatically. If an event is withdrawn, an amount already due stays until staff adjust it with a reason.

**SMN-16 · Agreed.** Staff may record a supplier invoice reference against any amount due.

**SMN-17 · Agreed.** A payment follow-up task is created when a schedule line is created, showing its date or trigger (SUP-23), and is completed when the amount due is settled by payment, credit or offset.

### **Payments to suppliers**

**SMN-18 · Agreed. Staff manually record a supplier payment with the supplier, date, amount and currency, and method. They record a payee account, bank or card reference, and supplier invoice reference when needed or known, along with who recorded the payment. A simple payment record does not require full payee-account setup. Only staff with the financial permission record supplier payments (ACC-10), with no approval step.**

**SMN-19 · Agreed.** One payment may settle several amounts due, and one amount due may be settled by several payments. Applications link each payment amount to what it settles.

**SMN-20 · Agreed. For MVP, staff may apply a supplier payment directly to the selected amount due or leave it unapplied. Automated matching by invoice reference, payee account or across Departures is a later enhancement; payments never cross suppliers.**

**SMN-21 · Agreed.** Staff may apply a payment to a schedule line that is not yet due; doing so posts that amount due immediately, marked paid early.

**SMN-22 · Agreed.** Any part of a payment that exceeds the amounts due it is applied to stays as an unapplied supplier payment on the payee account until staff apply it or the supplier refunds it.

**SMN-23 · Agreed.** A returned, cancelled or mistaken payment is corrected by a reversal with a reason, never by editing. The reversal reopens the amounts the payment had settled and keeps both records.

**SMN-24 · Agreed.** A net settlement records the gross amount, the actual cash and each fee, commission, credit or other offset that explains the difference. An offset is applied to a balance only where the settlement explicitly says it discharges that balance.

**SMN-25 · Agreed.** Each supplier obligation keeps its contract currency and each payment its actual currency. When they differ, staff record the conversion rate, date and converted amount, and bank or supplier fees separately. Any remaining difference stays open unless staff record an explicit adjustment (see Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout).

### **Funds held for another party**

**SMN-26 · Agreed.** Supplier payables, supplier receivables (including commission, refunds and credits), supplier-held funds due to the agency, and agency-held funds awaiting remittance to a supplier are separate open balances. They are never netted against each other automatically, and held funds are never assumed to equal a payable; an unmatched amount stays open in its own balance.

**SMN-27 · Agreed.** When a supplier collects a client payment for a charge owed to the agency, the client's charge is settled and a supplier-held funds balance is created for the amount. It is not agency cash, and no agency receipt is issued. How the supplier deals with the funds (remitting them, applying them against an amount the agency owes, or another documented resolution) is recorded as separate settlement applications.

**SMN-28 · Agreed. An optional later shortcut may bundle a supplier-confirmed client payment, supplier-held funds and an offset against a named amount due. MVP can record the supplier-direct client payment and supplier-held balance first, then record any confirmed offset separately. Staff rely on the supplier's statement or confirmation for an offset.**

**SMN-29 · Agreed.** When staff apply a payment to a supplier that holds client funds for the agency, the proposal shows those funds as an available offset, applied only when staff confirm the supplier's statement says so.

**SMN-30 · Agreed.** When the agency collects a client payment on a supplier's behalf, the amount is held as agency-held funds awaiting remittance, separate from the agency's own payables. A remittance that uses those funds is applied to both the held-funds balance and any payable it settles.

**SMN-31 · Agreed.** Where collected funds overlap with a commission receivable, the records are linked and the funds are applied once, keeping the commission's attribution without counting the amount twice.

### **Supplier commission**

**SMN-32 · Agreed.** An expected commission is created when a client booking is confirmed, for services drawn from a supplier arrangement, or when a standalone supplier reservation is confirmed. Staff enter the amount, or the commissionable amount and rate the supplier states (SUP-21), and the system multiplies them. It is attributed to the service that earned it.

**SMN-33 · Agreed.** A commission is expected, then confirmed or due, then received in cash, applied as an offset, or adjusted or reversed, with the transitions in the commission lifecycle table.

**SMN-34 · Agreed.** Commission withheld from an agency payment to the supplier is a non-cash offset that reduces both the commission receivable and the payable, with the gross obligation, commission and net cash kept separate.

**SMN-35 · Agreed for a later phase. Staff record one commission receipt for each supplier payment and apply it line by line, with the system proposing matches from supplier confirmation numbers. Adjustments, disputed differences, unapplied amounts and receipts across several Departures remain traceable. MVP may record an expected commission without requiring this matching workflow.**

**SMN-36 · Agreed.** When a booking is cancelled, staff reduce or reverse its expected commission. Commission a supplier pays on a cancellation penalty is entered as its own expected amount.

**SMN-37 · Agreed.** A clawback of commission already received is recorded as a reversal, and the amount becomes owed back to the supplier, paid or offset against a later payment.

**SMN-38 · Agreed.** Volume overrides and annual bonuses earned at supplier-agreement level are recorded manually as receivables on the supplier account, not attributed to any Departure. Calculating them automatically is deferred.

**SMN-39 · Agreed. An unconfirmed or unquantified expected commission blocks Departure reconciliation. Once the supplier confirms the amount and it is attributed to the service, staff may carry the unpaid amount as a linked supplier receivable and follow-up item without blocking reconciliation. It remains excluded from actual margin until received or offset (DEP-44, FIN-20).**

### **Deposit dispositions and supplier credits**

**SMN-40 · Agreed. When a reduction or cancellation affects a paid deposit, staff record the supplier-confirmed outcome as a refund due, a supplier credit, an amount retained as a cancellation charge, or a pending supplier decision, and may split the deposit among them. Amounts and supplier evidence are entered manually; a basic cancellation tier proposes the penalty for staff to verify (SUP-20). A confirmed, quantified and attributed supplier refund may remain unpaid as a linked supplier receivable and follow-up item without blocking Departure reconciliation; it does not reduce actual supplier cost or margin until received or offset. An unresolved supplier decision or unknown amount remains a blocker.**

**SMN-41 · Agreed.** Deposit terms (refundable, partly refundable, nonrefundable or credited toward a later balance) describe what happens on cancellation or reduction. They never change the distinction between what is committed, what is due and what is paid.

**SMN-42 · Agreed.** A supplier credit records the supplier and payee account, amount and currency, what it came from, the supplier's reference, the issue date, an optional expiry date, and restrictions in plain language.

**SMN-43 · Agreed.** A supplier credit is Open, Partly applied, Fully applied, Refunded or Expired, with the transitions in the supplier credit lifecycle table.

**SMN-44 · Agreed.** Applying a credit is a separate settlement application with an amount, date and supplier reference. It may settle an amount due under another arrangement only within the same supplier and payee account and where the supplier's terms permit. Partial applications are allowed and any remainder stays open. A credit is never applied across suppliers or treated as commission.

**SMN-45 · Agreed for a later phase. A supplier credit approaching expiry is flagged in the single staff task queue 60 days ahead by default and again on its expiry date. Staff record supplier-confirmed extensions with a reference and preserve the history.**

**SMN-46 · Agreed. At issue, a confirmed supplier credit counts as a recovery for the Departure it came from. An unused credit with a recorded disposition remains visible in Departure follow-up but does not block reconciliation. A credit used on another Departure is a means of paying that Departure's amount due, not a change to its cost. A credit that expires unused is recorded as an agency loss at supplier-account level, without reopening the original Departure.**

**SMN-47 · Agreed.** A credit a supplier issues in a traveler's name, such as a future cruise credit, is recorded on the traveler's profile for information, with its amount, expiry and restrictions. It is never an agency balance and cannot settle an agency amount due.

### **Contingent costs**

**SMN-48 · Agreed.** Contingent supplier costs stay separate from committed costs and amounts due. An internal estimate may use booked pickup from confirmed allocations, excluding holds and cancelled bookings, and is labelled as an estimate that creates no payable. To confirm an obligation, staff record the supplier-reported qualifying quantity, date and reference, then the supplier-confirmed amount. A fixed charter price is already committed, so unsold seats affect projected margin rather than adding a contingent cost.

### **Supplier account view**

**SMN-49 · Agreed. The target supplier account view shows open balances separately by payee account and currency, without a net figure. For MVP, staff can see and act on the open supplier items from each Departure; the consolidated supplier account view and broad financial reporting may be phased later.**

**SMN-50 · Agreed.** The account view lists schedule lines due in the next 90 days and lines waiting on an event, which show their trigger instead of a date. Every line links to its Departure or standalone Client Trip, and the history lists payments, receipts, credits, adjustments and statements, newest first.

**SMN-51 · Agreed. Staff may record a supplier statement with its date, the supplier's stated balance and the document. The target model shows the agency's balance as of that date and the difference, and staff record an explanation. Line-by-line matching of statements is deferred; commission statement matching (SMN-35) is also outside MVP.**

**SMN-52 · Agreed.** Each Departure shows the same records for its own arrangements: cost lines, schedule lines, amounts due, payments and commission. These are views of the same records, never copies.

**SMN-53 · Agreed.** Only staff with the financial permission can see supplier accounts, with the exact scope defined under ACC-11. Resellers never see them.

## **Scenarios**

**SC-SMN-01.** Given a committed cost line of \$20,000 with schedule lines of \$5,000 on signing and \$15,000 at 90 days before departure, when the arrangement is confirmed, then \$5,000 is posted as due, and nothing else is due until the 90-day date. Tests SMN-01, SMN-04, SMN-05.

Tests SMN-20.**

**SC-SMN-08.** Given a cruise line holding \$500 of client funds for the agency, when staff record a \$3,300 payment against a \$3,800 cabin balance, then the \$500 is offered as an offset and applied only after staff confirm the cruise line's statement shows it. Tests SMN-29.

**SC-SMN-09.** Given a \$4,200 commission receipt listing 18 bookings across three Departures, when one line pays \$40 less than expected and another cannot be matched, then staff record an adjustment with a reason for the first, and the second stays as an unapplied commission receipt. **SC-SMN-15.** Given a supplier holding \$500 of client funds for the agency and the agency owing it \$3,800, when staff open the supplier account, then both balances are shown separately and no net figure is shown. Tests SMN-26, SMN-49.

# **Client money**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. **Review** marks a rule that needs the agency's accountant or legal adviser. Rule prefix: **CMN**.

## **Context**

Each client booking has one charge built from its accepted price and terms. Lines preserve who is owed and the expected collection route; installments show when payment is expected. The Client Trip shows one receivable across its bookings. For MVP, staff can record a straightforward client payment or supplier obligation with defaults, without setting up a full accounting path.

Clients pay the agency, a supplier, or both, so every payment records its route, and the same payment means different things depending on who was owed. Only staff record money. Refunds follow the money back to whoever paid, and credits never sit unnoticed. Three audiences see a trip's money differently: staff see everything, a reseller sees the client side of its own bookings, and the client sees a statement with nothing about supplier costs or commission.

Bookings, optional client summaries and client terms are defined in Bookings and packages; supplier-held and agency-held funds in Supplier money; margin, currency and tax in Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout; payment reminders, receipt numbering and document formats in Documents and communications.

## **Rules**

### **Client charges**

**CMN-01 · Agreed.** Each client booking has one client charge, made up of charge lines of these types:

| Line type | Example |
| :---- | :---- |
| Traveler price | Ana, 1st/2nd guest, balcony, \$1,900 |
| Add-on | Shore excursion for two travelers, \$240 |
| Fee | Name-change fee, \$50; supplier fee passed through, \$100; agency card fee |
| Adjustment | Own-arranged air credit, \$300; agency discount; price reduction |
| Cancellation | Cancellation charge under the schedule, and credits reversing cancelled travelers' lines |
| Tax | Taxes recorded under the tax rules |

**CMN-02 · Agreed.** Each line records its type, the traveler it relates to if any, the amount, its creditor (agency or supplier) and its expected collection route (agency or supplier).

**CMN-03 · Agreed. Lines are created from the confirmed booking's accepted price snapshot (BKG-25). After confirmation, lines are never edited; amendments, fees, cancellations and price reductions add new lines.**

**CMN-04 · Agreed.** A line's creditor and collection route are separate facts, and neither is determined automatically by the agency's role as agent or principal.

**CMN-05 · Agreed.** Where a package price is owed to different creditors, its lines record explicit amounts that add up to the accepted package price. Lines are split only as needed to distinguish creditor, collection route, due timing or refund responsibility. These splits track balances and settlement, not revenue (see Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout).

**CMN-06 · Agreed. The complete amount still due from the client across a Client Trip's bookings and collection routes is the agency receivable. A payment made directly to a supplier reduces this amount when recorded, but is never agency cash or an agency receipt. Credits and refunds due are shown separately. The payment route and any supplier-held funds due to the agency remain visible.**

**CMN-07 · Agreed.** An amount that recovers a supplier deposit the agency paid is part of the agreed charge and is collected through the ordinary installments. It is never an extra charge, and a client payment toward it never creates a supplier payment. The line may link to the source deposit for traceability.

### **Installments**

**CMN-08 · Agreed.** Each charge has installments that together cover its total. Each installment has an amount, a due date and an expected collection route.

**CMN-09 · Agreed. The client terms include a payment schedule template, set as an agency default with an optional Departure override, such as a deposit of a fixed amount per traveler or a percentage, and the balance due a set number of days before departure. The system proposes installments from it when staff prepare a booking for client acceptance; staff may adjust them; the accepted schedule is snapshotted with the terms. For bookings without a Departure, staff set the schedule from the supplier deadlines.**

**CMN-10 · Agreed.** Installment due dates follow the supplier deadlines for the booking's services, with an agency buffer (14 days by default, with an optional Departure override) before each. Staff may combine amounts into fewer installments when each supplier deadline is still met.

**CMN-11 · Agreed.** For each supplier amount due that affects a booking's services, the system compares what the client is scheduled to have paid by that supplier date, less the buffer, with the booking's attributed share of the supplier amount. A shortfall is flagged as a warning. Client installments are never calculated from supplier costs.

**CMN-12 · Agreed.** Installments dated before a booking's confirmation become due on confirmation, combined into one amount.

**CMN-13 · Agreed.** Lines added after confirmation are due immediately by default. Staff may instead add them to the next installment or give them their own due date.

**CMN-14 · Agreed.** A credit that reduces the charge reduces the latest unpaid installments first.

**CMN-15 · Agreed.** Moving a due date later or reducing an installment needs no client acceptance. Moving a date earlier or increasing an installment needs the client's acceptance (BKG-42).

**CMN-16 · Agreed.** Accepted due dates never move silently. When a supplier deadline change affects them, a review item is created (DEP-12) and staff amend the dates explicitly.

**CMN-17 · Agreed.** The agency card fee is never scheduled; it is added as a fee line when an eligible card payment is made (CMN-32).

### **Recording payments**

**CMN-18 · Agreed. Staff manually record a client payment with the payer, date, amount and currency, and collection route. They record the method and reference when known; a supplier-direct payment identifies the supplier and its reference when known. The record shows who entered it. A supplier-direct payment reduces the Client Trip receivable but creates no agency cash or agency receipt. The client pays outside DepartureDesk; there is no payment-processing integration in MVP.**

**CMN-19 · Agreed.** Only staff with the financial permission enter or change payment, receipt, application and refund records, including payments made directly to a supplier. A reseller may give staff the details of a payment already made (amount, date, reference), never card details for the agency to process.

**CMN-20 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk never stores card numbers or security codes. For a card payment it keeps only the card brand, the last four digits and the processor's transaction reference. There is no processor integration in MVP.

**CMN-21 · Agreed.** The system proposes applying a payment to the oldest unpaid installment first, and staff review the proposal before saving. One payment may cover several installments, including future ones, and one installment may receive several payments.

**CMN-22 · Agreed.** A payment or credit may be applied to any booking on its Client Trip. Applying it to another Client Trip needs the recorded consent of the source trip's responsible client. Any client may pay toward a trip whose responsible client is someone else.

**CMN-23 · Agreed.** A payment that exceeds the installments it is applied to, or arrives without a designated charge, is held as unapplied credit on the Client Trip.

**CMN-24 · Agreed.** A payment received before a booking is confirmed is held as unapplied credit on the Client Trip. On confirmation, the system proposes applying it to the deposit installment, and staff confirm.

**CMN-25 · Agreed.** A payment covering several Departures is recorded once and divided between them, with a separate receipt for each Departure's share linked to the one payment.

**CMN-26 · Agreed. In MVP, a payment records its actual amount and currency; staff can apply it directly when it matches the Departure's client currency. Cross-currency conversion details (rate, date, converted amount and any remaining difference) are a later phase.**

**CMN-27 · Agreed.** A bounced check or failed transfer is reversed with a reason, never edited. The reversal reopens the installments the payment had settled, and both records are kept.

### **Collection routes**

**CMN-28 · Agreed.** A charge's balance shows the total, the amount paid to the agency, the amount paid to suppliers and the remainder.

**CMN-29 · Agreed. The collection route identifies whether money reached the agency or supplier. The Client Trip receivable is reduced by either route; only money collected by the agency is agency cash and eligible for an agency receipt:**

| Line owed to | Paid to the agency | Paid to the supplier |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Agency | Agency cash; agency receipt issued | Reduces the Client Trip receivable; creates supplier-held funds due to the agency (SMN-27); no agency cash or receipt |
| Supplier | Agency-held funds awaiting remittance (SMN-30) | Reduces the Client Trip receivable; no agency cash or agency receipt |

**CMN-30 · Agreed.** When a payment arrives by a different route than its installment expected, staff are shown the difference.

### **Agency card fee**

**CMN-31 · Agreed.** The agency card fee is a percentage set as an agency default with an optional Departure override. An unset Departure value uses the default, and an explicit 0% waives the fee for that Departure. Before enabling it, the agency must confirm its processor, card-network and legal requirements; the configured percentage is not a compliance determination.

**CMN-32 · Agreed.** The fee applies only to agency-processed card payments the agency has confirmed are eligible, as disclosed in the accepted terms (BKG-30), and never when eligibility is unknown. It is calculated on the card payment amount before the fee. Staff record the fee charged and the percentage used, snapshotted on the payment, and may waive or adjust it. A later policy change affects a confirmed booking only if its accepted terms allow it; otherwise it needs an amendment and the client's acceptance.

**CMN-33 · Agreed.** On a card refund, the client fee is refunded in proportion to the refunded amount, unless staff record an explicit adjustment consistent with the accepted terms. The processor's actual fee, including any it keeps after a refund, is recorded separately as an agency expense and never netted against the client's payment or refund.

**CMN-34 · Deferred.** Supplier-imposed payment fees charged directly to clients.

### **Net settlements and chargebacks**

**CMN-35 · Agreed.** A net settlement records the gross client payment, the actual cash and each fee or offset that explains the difference. For example, a \$1,000 card payment with a \$30 processor fee records a \$1,000 client payment, a \$30 agency expense and a \$970 bank deposit. Staff enter settlement details manually.

**CMN-36 · Agreed.** A chargeback on an agency-processed payment is recorded against the original payment. When the processor withdraws the funds, the affected payment application is reversed and the amount reopens on the charge, marked disputed. If the agency wins, the returned funds are a new settlement applied to the charge. If it loses, the amount stays due unless staff record a client adjustment. Processor chargeback fees are agency expenses. Chargebacks on supplier-direct payments may be recorded for visibility but create no agency cash movement.

### **Refunds and credits**

**CMN-37 · Agreed.** Staff enter client refund and credit amounts under the client's accepted terms. They are never derived automatically from a supplier refund or penalty, and client, supplier and inventory outcomes stay independent.

**CMN-38 · Agreed.** Once staff confirm a refund amount and who will issue it, they record a refund due on the Client Trip, with the staff member who authorized it (one authorization, by staff with the financial permission) and an expected date when known. It stays open until the money is actually returned, through one or more refund payments, each recording who issued it, who received it, the amount, the date and the route.

**CMN-39 · Agreed.** A refund goes by default to the payer who made the payment, by the same method. Staff may direct it elsewhere only with a recorded reason.

**CMN-40 · Agreed.** By default, each party refunds what it collected: the agency its portion and the supplier its own. Staff may change the split with a reason. A supplier refund paid directly to the client settles the refund due without agency cash; an agency refund is agency cash out.

**CMN-41 · Agreed.** Card refunds are processed outside DepartureDesk. Staff record each with the processor's reference, and any processor fee the agency does not recover is recorded as an agency expense.

**CMN-42 · Agreed. A credit from a cancellation, price reduction or overpayment stays on the Client Trip until staff apply it, refund it or turn it into a travel credit. An unapplied trip credit is an open item that blocks the Departure's reconciliation (DEP-44); the target model flags it after 30 days. In MVP, follow-up appears in the agency's single staff task queue.**

**CMN-43 · Agreed.** A travel credit belongs to the client whose payment created it and is not tied to the original Client Trip or Departure. It may be used on any later Client Trip where that client is the responsible client or a payer; the client's acceptance of the credit is the consent CMN-22 requires. Travel credits do not block any Departure's reconciliation.

**CMN-44 · Agreed.** A travel credit may have an expiry date, set by the client terms or at issue. It is flagged as expiry approaches, 60 days ahead by default, and staff record the expiry.

**CMN-45 · Review.** How the agency treats a travel credit that expires unused.

### **Overdue payments**

**CMN-46 · Agreed.** An installment is overdue from the day after its due date, in agency time. A partly paid installment is overdue for its unpaid remainder. There is no separate grace period.

**CMN-47 · Agreed. Each overdue installment appears in the single staff task queue with the days overdue and the next supplier deadline it puts at risk (CMN-11), and the booking owner is notified. An overdue deposit also flags the booking in Needs attention as space at risk (BKG-46).**

**CMN-48 · Agreed.** When an overdue installment was expected to be paid to a supplier, no automatic reminder goes to the client; instead a task asks staff to check the supplier's records.

**CMN-49 · Agreed.** A booking cancelled for non-payment records the initiator Non-payment. The client's cancellation schedule applies, as for a client-requested cancellation, while the record shows the agency took the action. This adds a fourth initiator to DEP-22.

**CMN-50 · Deferred.** Late payment fees.

### **Views, statements and receipts**

**CMN-51 · Agreed. Staff see a trip's full money picture: charge total; amounts paid to the agency and suppliers; credits; refunds due; and the amount still due from the client, which is the agency receivable. Installments show status and expected route. A chronological timeline includes due dates, actual payments, supplier obligations and commission linked to the trip's services, plus projected margin (see Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout). These views keep the Client Trip receivable, agency cash, supplier obligations, supplier-held funds, agency-held funds, credits, refunds due and commission receivables distinct.**

**CMN-52 · Agreed.** For bookings assigned to it, a reseller sees charge lines, installments, payments and the balance, but never supplier costs, commission or margin.

**CMN-53 · Agreed.** A client statement, addressed to the responsible client (CLT-02), shows charges by booking with their lines, payments labelled as paid to the agency or to the named supplier, credits, refunds, the balance due, and upcoming installments with where to pay. It never shows supplier costs, commission, margin, or other clients' bookings sharing a unit.

**CMN-54 · Agreed.** A client-account statement covers all of a client's trips, showing those with an open balance by default and all trips on request. Statements are never receipts.

**CMN-55 · Agreed.** An agency receipt is issued only for money the agency collected, never for a payment made directly to a supplier. Each receipt covers one Departure, or one Client Trip with no Departure, and may include several charges or installments on it. It shows the payer, the amount and currency received or allocated, the payment date, the collector and route, the payment reference and the amounts applied. Issued receipts are kept; corrections, reversals and refunds are recorded separately.

**CMN-56 · Agreed.** Statements go to the responsible client. Receipts go to the payer, for their own payments only.

**CMN-57 · Deferred.** Trust accounting for client funds, formal revenue recognition and accounting-system exports (see OVR-01).

# **Profitability, currency, tax, and closeout**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. **Review** marks a rule that needs the agency's accountant. Rule prefix: **FIN**.

## **Context**

This area defines how DepartureDesk reports what a trip earns. Margin is the agency's own income from a trip less what it costs, with commission counted once and money that only passes through, such as taxes and charges owed to suppliers, left out. The Departure is the reliable unit of profitability; booking-level figures are a view, and shared costs reach bookings only through an explicit, labelled allocation.

Before its first financial reconciliation, a Departure shows projected profitability and the inputs behind it. Reconciliation publishes an actual margin snapshot based on settled and otherwise final facts; confirmed unpaid supplier receivables remain visible separately and are excluded until received or offset. If a late material adjustment arrives, the prior snapshot remains available while it is reviewed, and re-reconciliation publishes a revised snapshot. Foreign currencies use recorded rates, never guesses; income or losses belonging to no Departure stay at agency level. DepartureDesk reports trip profitability for running the business; accounting treatment stays with the agency's accountant (OVR-01).

Charge lines and collection routes are defined in Client money; cost lines, commission and supplier credits in Supplier money; reconciliation and closeout in Departures (DEP-44 to DEP-46).

## **Rules**

### **Margin**

**FIN-01 · Agreed.** Margin is income less cost:

| Counts as | Items |
| :---- | :---- |
| Income | Client charge lines owed to the agency (traveler prices, add-ons, agency fees including change and card fees, cancellation charges), less adjustments, credits and refunds; commission on services where the client owes the supplier |
| Cost | Supplier costs net of the commission earned on them; pass-through supplier fees; penalties the supplier keeps; contingent costs once the supplier confirms them; trip-related agency expenses such as processor fees, chargeback fees and amounts the agency absorbs |
| Excluded | Taxes collected or paid on another party's behalf; client charge lines owed to suppliers; projected values of group benefits |

**FIN-02 · Agreed.** For a service where the client owes the supplier, margin is the commission. For a service the agency sells as principal, margin is the price less the net cost. A package mixing both adds them together.

**FIN-03 · Agreed.** Commission is counted once: as a reduction of net supplier cost, or as the income from a supplier-owed service, never also as separate margin income.

**FIN-04 · Agreed.** Profitability shows two figures: margin, and margin after seller commission.

**FIN-05 · Agreed.** Each booking records its seller of record separately from its booking owner. Seller commission is a manually entered payable attributed to the seller of record, with an amount and due date, which staff mark payable and then paid. Reassigning the booking owner never changes the seller of record or the commission. Changes after an amendment or cancellation are separate adjustments that keep the original history.

**FIN-06 · Deferred.** Seller commission plans, tiers, periodic statements, automatic earning rules and clawbacks.

### **Attribution and shared costs**

**FIN-07 · Agreed.** Applying a payment settles obligations; it never decides which booking a cost or commission belongs to.

**FIN-08 · Agreed.** A cost or commission is attributed to its source when known: a client booking, a unit shell, or a supplier arrangement and its Departure.

**FIN-09 · Agreed.** A package's price stays at booking level and is never split into revenue per service. Separately priced add-ons and explicit component markups are attributed to their service. Charge lines split by creditor (CMN-05) are not revenue allocations. Package revenue is never reallocated as occupancy changes.

**FIN-10 · Agreed.** A unit's cost is attributed to the bookings sharing it in proportion to the positions each occupies, labelled as allocated, at all times. The share of any unoccupied position stays at Departure level.

**FIN-11 · Agreed.** Costs and commission shared by the whole Departure, such as a coach, a guide or the escort's travel, stay at Departure level unless allocated at closeout (FIN-14).

**FIN-12 · Agreed.** In MVP, a shared cost may be allocated per confirmed traveler (the default) or per sellable unit.

**FIN-13 · Deferred.** Allocation by room-night or by commissionable booking value.

**FIN-14 · Agreed.** At closeout, staff choose a basis for each shared cost line or leave it at Departure level. The cost is spread over the travelers or units whose bookings use the arrangement or choice it belongs to, or over everyone when it serves the whole Departure; cancelled travelers are never included. The result is snapshotted and labelled allocated. If the Departure is reopened and reconciled again, a new snapshot is taken and the earlier one kept.

**FIN-15 · Agreed.** Before closeout, a booking's projected margin includes unit-shared costs (FIN-10) but not Departure-level shared costs, and says so.

**FIN-16 · Agreed.** Unsold capacity, such as empty cabins in a committed block or unsold charter seats, always stays at Departure level and is never spread over the travelers who booked.

### **Projected and actual**

**FIN-17 · Agreed. Profitability is projected until the first Financially reconciled snapshot, then actual based on the final and settled facts included in that snapshot. Confirmed unpaid supplier receivables are shown separately and excluded from actual margin until received or offset. Reopening keeps the last actual snapshot available with an adjustment in progress; reconciliation publishes a revised actual snapshot and retains history (DEP-45, DEP-46).**

**FIN-18 · Agreed.** Projected margin counts:

- income from confirmed bookings only, never drafts, holds or proposals, less credits and refunds already recorded  
- every commission not reversed, whether expected, confirmed or received  
- committed cost lines in full, including unsold capacity, and estimated cost lines where no committed amount exists, marked as estimates  
- trip-related expenses recorded so far

**FIN-19 · Agreed.** Contingent costs stay out of the margin and are shown beside it as risk.

**FIN-20 · Agreed.** Actual margin counts the agency-owed lines as finally settled, the final supplier amounts after amendments and penalties, only commission received or offset, actual expenses, the recorded financial effect of group benefits, and shared costs as allocated by the closeout snapshot.

**FIN-21 · Agreed.** A group benefit's projected value is never counted. Its actual financial effect counts once recorded, such as a received tour-conductor credit reducing cost.

### **Currency**

**FIN-22 · Agreed.** Each Departure has one client currency for its prices and charges, and a Client Trip with no Departure uses the agency's default currency. A Departure cannot have more than one client currency in MVP.

**FIN-23 · Agreed.** Supplier obligations keep their contract currency and payments their actual currency. When a conversion is needed, staff record the rate, date and converted amount, keeping the original amounts, and record bank fees separately. A cross-currency settlement uses the recorded conversion, and any remaining difference stays open unless staff record an explicit adjustment.

**FIN-24 · Agreed.** A Departure's margin is calculated in its client currency. Agency-wide totals use the agency's reporting currency.

**FIN-25 · Agreed.** Staff record a planning rate on the Departure for each foreign currency it uses, dated and changeable with its history kept. Projected margin converts unpaid foreign amounts at the planning rate and labels them so.

**FIN-26 · Agreed.** Actual margin converts each foreign amount at the rate recorded on its actual payment. Exchange movements appear as the difference between planning and actual figures; no separate exchange gain or loss is calculated.

**FIN-27 · Agreed.** If a foreign amount has neither a planning rate nor a payment rate, margin is shown as incomplete, listing the amounts that could not be converted.

**FIN-28 · Agreed.** Where a Departure's client currency differs from the reporting currency, its margin is converted for agency totals at the planning rate before closeout and at a closeout rate staff record after.

**FIN-29 · Deferred.** Automatic rate feeds, daily revaluation and automatic exchange gain or loss calculation.

### **Cancellation-exposure view**

**FIN-30 · Agreed.** The cancellation-exposure view shows separate supplier and client facts for each affected booking, with a Departure roll-up, and never calculates a net agency loss.

**FIN-31 · Agreed.** Supplier facts are the contracted or guaranteed amount, the amount paid, the amount currently due, confirmed cancellation charges or nonrefundable deposits, and refunds or credits marked pending, confirmed, or received or applied. Client facts are accepted charges, payments made to the agency, payments made directly to the supplier, amounts still due, and credits or refunds due versus actually issued. Proposed, unaccepted price amendments are shown apart from accepted charges.

**FIN-32 · Agreed.** Each item shows its service or supplier, amount and currency, status, source and relevant deadline. Estimated or contingent penalties are kept apart from confirmed obligations, and a pending supplier refund or credit never reduces the amount currently due. Each category is totalled separately by currency, and supplier amounts are never netted against client charges or payments.

### **Outside any Departure**

**FIN-33 · Agreed.** A Client Trip with no Departure has its own margin under the same rules. It becomes actual automatically once all its services are in the past and it has no open financial items, and reverts to projected if a new item appears.

**FIN-34 · Agreed.** Income or losses that belong to no Departure or Client Trip are recorded as agency-level items, each with a type (override income, expired supplier credit, expired travel credit or other), an amount and currency, a date, a reference and a note. They count toward agency totals and never toward any Departure's margin.

**FIN-35 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk does not track general business costs such as rent, salaries or software; those stay in the accounting system.

**FIN-36 · Agreed.** For reporting periods, a Departure's margin is counted by its departure date, a standalone Client Trip's by its first service date, and an agency-level item by its recorded date. Accounting recognition is separate (FIN-39).

### **Tax**

**FIN-37 · Agreed.** Tax amounts are recorded with their source, without determining taxability or calculating jurisdiction-specific tax. Client-side taxes stay separate from taxes on supplier obligations. Where known, record the tax type or jurisdiction, the taxable basis, the rate or supplied amount, whether it is included or added, and who determines, collects and remits it. No separate tax-reviewed status is required, and tax display policy is reviewed with the agency's advisers.

**FIN-38 · Deferred.** Tax calculation.

### **Accounting boundary**

**FIN-39 · Review.** The agency's accountant confirms the treatment of mixed agent-and-principal packages, revenue-recognition timing, and any trust treatment of client funds. DepartureDesk records operational trip-level money but does not decide accounting treatment.

## **What projected and actual margin count**

| Item | Projected | Actual |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Client income | Confirmed bookings' agency-owed lines, less credits and refunds recorded | Agency-owed lines as finally settled |
| Commission | Expected, confirmed or received, unless reversed | Received or offset only |
| Supplier costs | Committed lines in full; estimates where nothing is committed, marked | Final amounts after amendments and penalties |
| Contingent costs | Shown beside margin as risk | Included once supplier-confirmed |
| Group benefits | Never counted | Recorded financial effect |
| Departure-level shared costs | At Departure level only | Allocated by the closeout snapshot, or left at Departure level |
| Foreign amounts | Planning rate, labelled | Payment rates |

# **Operations**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. Rule prefix: **OPS**.

## **Context**

Operations is how DepartureDesk keeps work from being missed. Staff deal with two kinds of item. A **task** is something a person must do by a date, such as submitting a rooming list or deciding go/no-go; it has an owner and is completed by a person. A **condition** is something currently wrong, such as an overdue installment or a capacity conflict; it is calculated from the records and disappears when the cause is fixed. Two further kinds of message support them: **reminders** say a task's date is approaching, and **notifications** say something happened.

Tasks belong to a person or to a group whose members claim them, so work is shared without anyone losing accountability. Conditions appear in two queues, one operational and one financial. When something goes wrong during travel, an incident record holds what happened, who is dealing with it and what was done.

The deadlines that create supplier tasks are defined in Suppliers and arrangements (SUP-22, SUP-23); the conditions themselves are defined in the areas that raise them, cited in the queue tables below.

## **Rules**

### **Tasks and conditions**

**OPS-01 · Agreed.** Work items are either tasks or conditions. A task is an action a person must take by a date. A condition is a situation that is currently wrong and lasts until it is resolved.

**OPS-02 · Agreed.** Every task uses one record, whatever created it. It records its source (supplier deadline, payment schedule line, go/no-go review date, supplier resubmission, supplier-route payment check, incident follow-up, condition or manual), the action required, the due date with time and time zone, the owner (an individual or a group), its status, a completion note or evidence, and a link to the record it concerns.

**OPS-03 · Agreed.** A task is Open, In progress, Completed or Waived, with the transitions in the task lifecycle table; overdue is worked out from the due date. A task is complete when the required action is recorded. It stays In progress while staff wait for a supplier response the action depends on, and is Waived, with a reason, when the deadline no longer applies. Completing a task changes no other record; staff make those changes through the relevant workflows.

**OPS-04 · Agreed.** When a deadline changes, the existing task is updated and its history kept. A deadline change alone never reopens a completed task whose action is done. If the same requirement becomes actionable again, the existing task is reopened with its earlier history; a distinct action or obligation gets a new task.

**OPS-05 · Agreed.** What completes a supplier deadline task depends on its type, as listed in SUP-22.

**OPS-06 · Agreed.** A condition is calculated from the records, never stored separately. It appears in a queue with its reason and a link, and clears itself when the underlying situation is resolved. Staff cannot dismiss a condition. The only exception is a review item, which staff clear with a note (DEP-14).

**OPS-07 · Agreed.** Staff may create a task from a condition, such as "chase the final payment". The task is assigned like any other and linked to the condition.

### **Ownership**

**OPS-08 · Agreed.** A task is assigned to an individual or to a group. A group is either a Departure team (ACC-09) or an agency work group.

**OPS-09 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin may define agency work groups, such as Finance or Air desk, as named lists of staff. A work group routes work only; it grants no access and limits none.

**OPS-10 · Agreed.** A group task must be claimed by a member of the group before it can move to In progress; it then has that member as its owner. The owner may hand it back to the group.

**OPS-11 · Agreed.** A task the system creates is routed as follows:

| Task | Routed to |
| :---- | :---- |
| Payment task (SMN-17) or supplier-route payment check (CMN-48) | The agency's payments work group if defined; otherwise as for other Departure work |
| Supplier submission, deadline or resubmission, go/no-go review date, or other Departure work | The Departure's default task owner if set; otherwise the Departure team |
| Work on a Client Trip with no Departure | The booking's staff owner if the booking is agency-owned; otherwise the agency's default group |

**OPS-12 · Agreed.** A Departure may name a default task owner, one member of its Departure team. This is a routing convenience, not an owner role, and grants no access.

**OPS-13 · Agreed.** An unclaimed group task due within 7 days is a condition in Needs attention.

**OPS-14 · Agreed.** When a staff member leaves a group or is deactivated, the tasks they claimed return to their group, unclaimed and flagged under OPS-13. Tasks claimed by others are unaffected.

**OPS-15 · Agreed.** Reseller users never own tasks and are never group members. A task concerning a reseller-assigned booking is routed to the Departure team.

**OPS-16 · Agreed.** A task is shown to its owner, or to every member of its group, when it is created. Every assignment, claim, hand-back and reassignment is logged.

### **Reminders**

**OPS-17 · Agreed.** Reminder timing uses agency defaults for each deadline type, which a supplier arrangement may override. Manual tasks and review dates get one reminder the day before by default. Postponement offers follow DEP-31.

**OPS-18 · Agreed.** Reminders that would fall before a task existed are skipped. When a deadline changes, future reminders are recalculated, and reminders already sent stay in the task's history.

**OPS-19 · Agreed.** Reminders always appear in the app. Each staff member may also receive them by email, either immediately or in a daily digest of what is due today, due soon and overdue; the digest is the default.

**OPS-20 · Agreed.** A reminder goes to the task's owner, or to every member of its group if it is unclaimed. An overdue task is also visible in the team view. There is no automatic escalation.

**OPS-21 · Agreed.** Reminders go out at a fixed time of day in agency time, 8:00 a.m. by default as an agency setting. Weekends are not treated specially.

**OPS-22 · Deferred.** Snoozing reminders, and personal reminder schedules.

### **Notifications**

**OPS-23 · Agreed.** Only the events in the notifications table send a notification. A condition does not also notify anyone unless the table says so.

**OPS-24 · Agreed.** Staff see notifications in the app and may also receive them by email, immediately or in the daily digest. Reseller users see them in the app only; email to resellers is deferred. Notifications have read and unread status and are kept for 90 days.

### **Queues and summaries**

**OPS-25 · Agreed.** Staff see their tasks in My tasks by default, alongside their groups' tasks and a team view for monitoring and reassignment. The same task records appear in each Departure's task list, never as copies. Each row shows the action, deadline and cutoff time, supplier and Departure, owner, status, overdue state, and payment amount when known. Tasks can be filtered by overdue, due today, upcoming and completed, and opening one leads to its linked record. A calendar view is deferred.

**OPS-26 · Agreed.** The Needs attention queue lists the operational conditions in the Needs attention table and is seen by staff with the Departure-management permission.

**OPS-27 · Agreed. Financial follow-up conditions appear in the single staff task queue, with clear ownership and access based on the underlying financial permission. The Financial review table defines which conditions create or update a task; it is not a separate queue.**

**OPS-28 · Agreed.** Queues are sorted by urgency, meaning the date at risk, by default, and can be filtered by condition type, Departure, age, and "my Departures" (those where the staff member is on the Departure team). No composite risk score is calculated. Configurable dashboards and a large set of fixed reports are deferred.

**OPS-29 · Agreed.** Resellers do not see the queues. Conditions on their bookings are shown on the booking, and the events in the notifications table reach them as notifications.

**OPS-30 · Agreed.** Each Departure shows a summary: its summary label (DEP-04), confirmed travelers and units against each supplier minimum, held space, the next supplier deadlines, open review items, tasks and conditions, open financial items (DEP-44), and projected or actual margin with margin after seller commission (FIN-04).

### **Incidents during travel**

**OPS-31 · Agreed.** Staff record a problem during travel as an incident on a Departure or Client Trip, with when it happened, its type (missed connection, supplier failure, itinerary disruption, medical or emergency, or other), the affected travelers and services, a description, a dated log of actions, and a status of open or resolved with a resolution note.

**OPS-32 · Agreed.** An open incident is a condition in Needs attention until resolved, and staff may create tasks from it.

**OPS-33 · Agreed.** Consequences of an incident, such as amendments, client credits or refunds, supplier credits or goodwill gestures, are recorded through their normal workflows and linked to the incident. An undelivered inclusion links to its SUP-32 flag.

**OPS-34 · Agreed.** Compensation claimed from a supplier is recorded as a supplier receivable with the supplier's reference. While pending, it is an open financial item that keeps the Departure from being reconciled until it is settled or written off.

**OPS-35 · Agreed.** An incident shows staff the affected travelers' emergency and on-trip contacts. Passport details remain behind their own permission (CLT-18).

**OPS-36 · Agreed.** Reseller users see incidents on bookings assigned to them, read-only.

**OPS-37 · Agreed.** An open incident does not prevent a Departure from being marked Departed.

**OPS-38 · Deferred.** Traveler location tracking, mass messaging, 24-hour escalation and insurance claim handling. In MVP, an insurance claim is recorded only as a reference on the incident.

## **Scenarios**

**SC-OPS-01.** Given a rooming-list deadline on a Departure with no default task owner, when the arrangement is confirmed, then the task is created for the Departure team, shown to every member, and cannot move to In progress until one of them claims it. Tests OPS-10, OPS-11, OPS-16.

**SC-OPS-02.** Given an unclaimed group task due in 5 days, then it appears in Needs attention; when a member claims it, then the condition clears. Tests OPS-06, OPS-13.

**SC-OPS-03.** Given a payment schedule line on a Departure in an agency with a Finance work group, when the line is created, then its payment task goes to the Finance group. Tests OPS-11.

**SC-OPS-04.** Given a staff member who has claimed three tasks, when they are deactivated, then the three tasks return to their groups unclaimed and are flagged if due within 7 days. Tests OPS-14.

**SC-OPS-05.** Given a completed names-submission task, when the supplier moves the deadline later, then the task stays completed; when the supplier asks for the names to be resubmitted, then the same task reopens with its history. Tests OPS-04.

**SC-OPS-06.** Given a task waiting on the cruise line's reply before the agency can act, then it stays In progress; when the cruise line drops the requirement, then staff mark it Waived with a reason. Tests OPS-03.

**SC-OPS-07. Given an overdue installment, when staff open its task in the single staff task queue, then the task remains linked to the overdue condition until payment is recorded; staff may add a "chase payment" action without dismissing the condition. Tests OPS-06, OPS-07.**

**SC-OPS-08.** Given a staff member who chose the daily digest, when three of their tasks have reminders due, then they get one email at 8:00 a.m. agency time listing all three, and each reminder also appears in the app. Tests OPS-19, OPS-21.

**SC-OPS-09.** Given a supplier declines a hotel on a reseller's booking, then the reseller receives an in-app notification, and the declined service also appears in Needs attention for staff. Tests OPS-23, OPS-24, OPS-29.

**SC-OPS-10.** Given a reseller user, when they look for the Needs attention queue, then it is not available; the overdue installment on their booking is shown on the booking. Tests OPS-29.

**SC-OPS-11.** Given the coach company fails to send the airport coach, when staff record an incident, then it appears in Needs attention and shows the affected travelers' emergency and on-trip contacts; when staff claim \$600 compensation from the coach company, then a supplier receivable is recorded and the Departure cannot be reconciled until it is settled or written off. Tests OPS-31, OPS-32, OPS-34, OPS-35.

**SC-OPS-12.** Given an open incident on the last day of travel, when staff mark the Departure Departed, then it is allowed and the incident stays open. Tests OPS-37.

# **Documents and communications**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. Rule prefix: **DOC**.

## **Context**

DepartureDesk produces the documents clients and suppliers need, from optional client summaries and confirmations to receipts, statements, acknowledgment forms and rooming lists. Every issued document is a fixed snapshot: it never changes after issue, a correction becomes a new linked version, and every delivery is recorded. Clients have no login, so documents reach them as PDF attachments, with the key facts summarized in the email itself.

When a reseller owns a booking, its documents go to the reseller by default, because the client relationship is usually the reseller's. Payment reminders are sent automatically and calculated fresh each day, so payments and date changes take effect without anyone managing a schedule. Communication that happens outside the app, by phone or from staff inboxes, is recorded in a log so the whole history sits in one place.

The agency profile, inclusion flags and footers are defined in Agency setup (SET-04 to SET-07); the money shown on documents in Client money; what traveler data may appear in Clients and travelers (CLT-36).

## **Rules**

### **The document catalog**

**DOC-01 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk issues these client documents:

| Document | Issued by | Sent to by default | Covers |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Proposal | Staff or assigned reseller | Responsible client | An optional summary of one or more draft bookings as alternatives (BKG-37, BKG-38) |
| Booking confirmation | Staff or assigned reseller | Responsible client | One booking |
| Invoice | Staff or assigned reseller | Responsible client | One booking's charge and installments |
| Receipt | Staff or assigned reseller | The payer | That payer's payment, for one Departure (CMN-55) |
| Itinerary and statement | Staff or assigned reseller | Responsible client | One Client Trip |
| Client-account statement | Staff only | Responsible client | All of a client's trips, open by default (CMN-54) |
| Cancellation notice | Staff | Responsible client | Cancelled bookings or travelers |
| Postponement offer | Staff | Responsible client | Revised dates, price and terms (DEP-30) |
| Acknowledgment form | Staff or assigned reseller | Each traveler, or a guardian | A waiver or acknowledgment to sign (DOC-35) |

**DOC-02 · Agreed.** Each Client Trip has one combined itinerary and statement. When the schedule changes (DEP-39), it is reissued as a new version, not as a separate document.

**DOC-03 · Agreed.** Documents are built only from recorded data. Client documents never show traveler dates of birth, passport details, supplier identifiers or emergency contacts (CLT-36), nor supplier costs, commission or margin (CMN-53). Travelers on other bookings who share a unit appear by name only, if at all.

**DOC-04 · Agreed.** A cancellation notice states what was cancelled, the cancellation initiator (DEP-22, CMN-49), and the charges, credits and refunds under the accepted terms.

**DOC-05 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk generates the postponement offer document but does not send it. Staff deliver it themselves and record the delivery (DOC-21), so the recorded offer matches what the client received.

### **Recipients**

**DOC-06 · Agreed.** Documents go by default to the recipients in the catalog: the responsible client for most documents, the payer for a receipt (CMN-56), and each traveler, or a minor's parent or guardian contact, for an acknowledgment form.

**DOC-07 · Agreed.** On a booking owned by a reseller, every client document defaults to the reseller, and the sender may switch to the client or send to both. When staff send, the reseller is preselected; when a reseller user sends, the client is preselected, with the option to copy the reseller.

**DOC-08 · Agreed.** Each reseller relationship has a setting, "Agency may send documents directly to this reseller's clients", on by default. When it is off, staff may send documents on that reseller's bookings only to the reseller.

**DOC-09 · Agreed.** Documents on a reseller-owned booking carry a line such as "Booked through \[reseller name\]".

**DOC-10 · Agreed.** When a booking is reassigned, later documents follow the new owner; documents already sent are unchanged.

### **Issuing and versions**

**DOC-11 · Agreed.** Each issued document is kept as an immutable version with its exact rendered content and displayed values, including agency profile fields, inclusion flags and footers. Later profile or footer changes apply only to future issues.

**DOC-12 · Agreed.** Reissuing a changed document creates a linked next version with a recorded reason, and marks the earlier version superseded. Both are kept.

**DOC-13 · Agreed.** Resending the same version records a new delivery and creates no new version.

**DOC-14 · Agreed.** Receipts are immutable payment evidence. A presentation-only correction is issued as a linked, clearly labelled corrected copy, keeping the original. A wrong payment fact is corrected by a linked reversal or adjustment, followed by a new receipt for the corrected record if needed. A receipt is never overwritten.

### **Sending**

**DOC-15 · Agreed.** Emails come from the agency's unique sender address on a DepartureDesk-managed domain, showing the agency's display name; agencies provide no mail-server credentials. An Agency Admin may set an optional agency-wide Reply-To address, separate from the contact email shown on documents. Replies go to it when set; when unset, the sender address accepts no replies.

**DOC-16 · Agreed.** Each document is sent as a PDF attachment. Secure no-login document links are deferred, to be built with BKG-43 and CLT-26.

**DOC-17 · Agreed.** The email body carries a short summary of the document's key facts, as in the email summary table, plus the references and any personal note from the sender. It states that the attached document is the official version, is generated from the same snapshot, and is saved with the delivery record. It never contains traveler personal details.

**DOC-18 · Agreed.** Each recipient receives a separate email, recorded as a separate delivery.

**DOC-19 · Agreed.** Every delivery records the document and version, the recipient, the sender address, who sent it (a user, or the system for scheduled reminders), the time, the outcome (sent, failed or bounced) and any personal note. Each send is one logical delivery, not one record per technical retry.

**DOC-20 · Agreed.** When a send fails or bounces, the booking owner is notified (see Operations), and staff resend the same version once the address is fixed.

**DOC-21 · Agreed.** Staff and resellers may download any document they can send. When they deliver it outside the app, they may record a manual delivery with the method (own email, printed or in person), the recipient and the date, marked as manual.

**DOC-22 · Agreed.** Emails carry no tracking pixel. Engagement is shown through delivery outcomes and recorded responses such as acceptances and payments. Tracking document views is deferred until secure links exist.

**DOC-23 · Agreed.** Documents are in English, with amounts in the Departure's client currency. Other languages are deferred.

**DOC-24 · Agreed.** A reseller may issue and send documents only for bookings assigned to it, and any statement a reseller issues includes only those bookings. Only staff issue client-account statements, cancellation notices and postponement offers.

### **Client payment reminders**

**DOC-25 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin turns on any combination of reminders at 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before a due date; 7 and 1 are on by default. Reminders go at a fixed time in agency time, set for the whole agency, with no Departure or booking overrides.

**DOC-26 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin may also turn on overdue reminders at 1 and 7 days after a due date; the 1-day reminder is on by default.

**DOC-27 · Agreed.** Reminders are calculated at each send time, not stored in advance: the system sends a reminder for each installment with an unpaid remainder whose due date is exactly an enabled number of days away, or past. Payments, due date changes and schedule changes therefore take effect from the next send.

**DOC-28 · Agreed.** A reminder goes to the reseller that owns the booking; if none, to the responsible client's billing contact (CLT-22), or its preferred email when no billing contact is set. It never goes to both the reseller and the client.

**DOC-29 · Agreed.** A reminder states the booking and Client Trip references, the amount still owed and its due date, and where to pay: for an installment expected via a supplier, the supplier's name and the booking reference to quote; otherwise, the agency's "How to pay" text. It is an email with no attachment and never includes traveler personal details.

**DOC-30 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin maintains a "How to pay" text, such as bank details or a payment link, used in reminders.

**DOC-31 · Agreed.** When several installments on one Client Trip fall on the same reminder day, the recipient receives one email listing them all.

**DOC-32 · Agreed.** No reminder is sent to an address marked undeliverable (CLT-21), or for an amount reopened by a chargeback still in dispute. An overdue installment expected via a supplier gets a staff task instead of a reminder (CMN-48).

**DOC-33 · Agreed.** There is no switch to pause reminders. To agree a delay with a client, staff move the due date (CMN-15), and reminders follow it.

**DOC-34 · Agreed.** Client payment reminders are separate from staff task reminders and postponement reminders. Each is logged with the system as actor and the agency's sender address.

### **Waivers and acknowledgments**

**DOC-35 · Agreed.** The agency keeps a library of acknowledgment templates, each with a title, its wording (typed or an uploaded PDF), who must sign, and a due date rule such as "30 days before departure".

**DOC-36 · Agreed.** A Departure or offering chooses which acknowledgments apply. An acknowledgment may apply only to travelers with a particular choice, such as those who did not take the insurance add-on.

**DOC-37 · Agreed.** Staff or the assigned reseller generate each traveler's form and send it. When it returns, staff record the method (signed copy or email reply), the date, who recorded it and the evidence, which is always required. The record keeps the version of the wording signed; a later template change leaves existing signatures in place, and staff request a new signature only where they choose.

**DOC-38 · Agreed.** A minor's acknowledgment is signed by their parent or guardian contact (CLT-22).

**DOC-39 · Agreed.** Each traveler's acknowledgment is Not sent, Sent, Signed or Waived, with the transitions in the acknowledgment lifecycle table. One still unsigned 7 days before its due date is a condition in Needs attention. A missing acknowledgment blocks nothing automatically.

**DOC-40 · Agreed.** Acknowledgment forms do not collect medical details in MVP; health-related needs stay on the specific service (CLT-07). Electronic signatures are deferred.

### **Supplier outputs**

**DOC-41 · Agreed.** For each supplier arrangement, staff can generate the supplier outputs in the supplier outputs table.

**DOC-42 · Agreed.** Before generating an output, staff choose which supplier-required fields it includes, such as date of birth, gender or passport number, and only those fields appear. Outputs never include client prices or other money.

**DOC-43 · Agreed.** Each output can be downloaded as CSV or PDF, or emailed from DepartureDesk. Before sending an output that contains passport details, staff confirm the recipient, and each traveler's passport share is logged (ACC-27).

**DOC-44 · Agreed.** Staff record each submission: a snapshot of what was sent (CLT-14), the recipient, the method (app email, supplier portal or other), the supplier's reference and the date. Recording it completes the follow-up task, or leaves it In progress while the supplier's acknowledgment is awaited (OPS-03).

**DOC-45 · Agreed.** After a submission, an output can show only what changed since the last submission.

### **Communication log**

**DOC-46 · Agreed.** Staff may record communication that happened outside the app on a Client Trip, booking, supplier arrangement or Departure: the date and time, direction, channel (phone, email, text, in person or other), the contact it was with, a summary and optional attachments.

**DOC-47 · Agreed.** Log entries and app-sent documents and reminders appear together in one chronological history.

**DOC-48 · Agreed.** Log entries are internal to staff by default. Staff may mark an entry as shared, making it visible to the reseller that owns the booking.

**DOC-49 · Agreed.** Log entries are never deleted, and an edit keeps the earlier wording.

**DOC-50 · Agreed.** Replies to app emails reach the agency's Reply-To mailbox and do not flow back into DepartureDesk; staff log the ones that matter.

### **Customization**

**DOC-51 · Agreed.** Each document type has one fixed layout. Agencies customize documents through the profile, inclusion flags and footers (SET-04 to SET-07), the "How to pay" text, senders' personal notes, and an optional agency logo.

**DOC-52 · Deferred.** Editable layouts, custom fields, custom document types, editable standard email wording, other languages, full reseller branding, supplier-specific output layouts, service vouchers, email integration, and secure links with view tracking.

# **Reporting and boundaries**

Draft area for the DepartureDesk Product Specification, for review. **Agreed** rules come from the sources listed under Migration notes or from our discussion of 28 October. Rule prefix: **RPT**.

## **Context**

The queues show what is wrong today, and the per-Departure, per-trip and per-supplier views show detail. Reports answer the agency-wide questions an owner asks every week: how Departures are doing, what was sold, who owes what, and what money the agency is holding. MVP has a small, fixed set of reports built entirely from rules already agreed, so there is nothing new to calculate. Agency users can export what they can see; resellers cannot export.

This area also states, in one place, what DepartureDesk will never do because another system or adviser owns it, and indexes every feature deferred beyond MVP so the roadmap starts from a complete list.

## **Rules**

### **The report set**

**RPT-01 · Agreed.** MVP has nine fixed reports, listed in the reports table. Each is a filterable table, with no charts and no report builder.

**RPT-02 · Agreed.** The client money held report shows, as of today, client payments the agency collected for Client Trips that have not yet travelled, less refunds, together with unapplied client credits and open travel credits, each shown separately. It is a total for the agency's information, not trust accounting (FIN-39).

**RPT-03 · Agreed.** Reports are built only from rules already agreed. The per-supplier, per-Departure and per-trip views defined in other areas remain where they are; reports provide the agency-wide totals.

### **Access**

**RPT-04 · Agreed.** Each report is seen by staff with the permission shown in the reports table.

**RPT-05 · Agreed.** Reseller users have no reports and no exports. On their bookings list they may see the seller commission the agency owes them, payable and paid, on screen only. They may still download an individual document they are allowed to send (DOC-21).

**RPT-06 · Agreed.** Every report can be filtered by office, but offices never limit access (SET-12).

### **Periods, filters and currency**

**RPT-07 · Agreed.** Each report states its date basis: Departure profitability by departure date (FIN-36); sales by confirmation date; money movements by transaction date; balances, amounts due, receivables, credits and money held as of today.

**RPT-08 · Deferred.** Reports showing balances as of a past date, such as month-end.

**RPT-09 · Agreed.** Every report can be filtered by Departure, office, supplier, reseller or seller, and currency, wherever the filter applies.

**RPT-10 · Agreed.** Each row shows its amount in its own currency, and totals are given per currency. A report may also show a combined total in the reporting currency, converted at the rates in FIN-28 and labelled as converted; amounts with no rate are left out of that total and listed.

### **Exports**

**RPT-11 · Agreed.** Agency users may export any report or list view as CSV. An export contains exactly what the user can see, with their filters applied. Printing a view uses the browser.

**RPT-12 · Agreed.** Staff with the financial permission may export every money movement in a period: client payments, refunds, supplier payments, commission receipts, offsets and adjustments, each with its date, type, amount, currency, counterparty, Departure or Client Trip, and references. It is raw data for the bookkeeper, not an accounting journal.

**RPT-13 · Agreed.** An Agency Admin may export the agency's core records as a set of CSV files: clients, travelers, Departures, bookings, charges, payments, suppliers and arrangements. Passport details are included only when the Agency Admin also holds the passport permission, and each traveler's export is then logged as a passport share (ACC-27). Passport images are never included in a bulk export.

**RPT-14 · Agreed.** Every export is audited with who ran it, what it covered, its filters and the number of rows.

**RPT-15 · Agreed.** Platform Administrators may export agency data only under an approved access grant (ACC-21).

**RPT-16 · Agreed.** Only Agency Admins may view or export their agency's audit history. This settles the first part of ACC-29; audit retention stays open.

### **Outside DepartureDesk by design**

**RPT-17 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk is not the agency's general ledger. Bank reconciliation, tax filing, revenue recognition and general business costs stay in the agency's accounting system (OVR-01, FIN-35, FIN-39).

**RPT-18 · Agreed.** DepartureDesk never processes or stores card data (CMN-20). Any future payment processor integration keeps card details with the processor.

**RPT-19 · Agreed.** Whether something is taxable, and at what rate, is decided by the agency's advisers; DepartureDesk records the outcome (FIN-37).

**RPT-20 · Agreed.** Whether a disclosure, waiver wording or retention period is legally sufficient is decided by the agency's legal adviser (SET-05, DOC-35, CLT-39).

**RPT-21 · Agreed.** When integrations are added, DepartureDesk remains the source of truth for trip-level money and the accounting system for the ledger. Integrations send summaries one way and never make DepartureDesk a second ledger.

### **Deferred features**

**RPT-22 · Deferred.** Pre-sale costing and break-even scenarios at several group sizes.

**RPT-23 · Agreed.** The deferred index below lists every deferred item by the rule that defers it, with a short name and no rule text. When a deferred item is brought into scope, its row leaves the index.

# **Questions for the agency's advisers**

Appendix to the DepartureDesk Product Specification, October 2026\.

DepartureDesk is an operational system for travel agencies that run group Departures and individual trips. It records what clients owe and pay, what the agency owes and pays suppliers, commission, credits and refunds, and it reports trip profitability. It is not the agency's accounting system: it sends raw transaction data to the bookkeeper (RPT-12) and leaves accounting treatment to the agency's accountant (RPT-17).

The specification marks five rules **Review** because they depend on accounting or legal judgment. For each, this appendix explains what DepartureDesk does now and what we need confirmed. The current behavior is a working assumption, not a recommendation of accounting or legal treatment.

## **For the accountant**

### **1\. Packages that mix agent and principal services (FIN-39)**

**Situation.** An agency often sells a package at one price, such as a cruise plus a land tour. For the cruise it usually acts as the cruise line's agent, earning commission; for the land tour it often acts as principal, buying services and reselling them at its own price. DepartureDesk records the agency's role for each service separately.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** For reporting margin, it counts the commission as the income from agent services, and the price less the net cost for principal services, adding the two for a mixed package (FIN-02). The client's invoice shows the single package price.

**What we need to know.**

- Should the agency's revenue from a mixed package be reported gross (the full package price) or net, or split between the two by service?  
- Does the agency need the split recorded or shown anywhere for accounting or tax, for example on invoices or in the transaction export?

### **2\. When revenue is recognized (FIN-39)**

**Situation.** Clients pay months before travel, and suppliers pay commission after it.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** It treats client money as held until travel, reports a Departure's margin by its departure date (FIN-36), and counts commission as actual only once received (FIN-20). It records no revenue-recognition entries.

**What we need to know.**

- When should the agency recognize revenue: at departure, at return, or at another point, such as final payment?  
- Is commission recognized when earned (at travel) or when received?  
- What would you need from the transaction export (RPT-12) to post this yourself?

### **3\. Client money held for future travel (FIN-39)**

**Situation.** Some US states' seller-of-travel rules require client payments to be held in trust or escrow until travel.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** It reports the total of client money the agency holds for trips not yet taken, with unapplied credits and travel credits shown separately (RPT-02). It does no trust accounting.

**What we need to know.**

- Do the agency's states require trust or escrow accounts?  
- Is the client money held report enough to support your reconciliation, or do you need more detail, such as per-client balances or the dates money was received?

### **4\. Supplier credits (SMN-46)**

**Situation.** When a booking is cancelled, a supplier often gives the agency a credit toward future bookings instead of a refund, typically usable for 12 to 24 months.

**What DepartureDesk does now.**

- When the credit is issued, it counts as a recovery for the Departure it came from, much like a refund.  
- An unused credit does not stop that Departure from being reconciled.  
- When the credit is used on another Departure, it is treated as a way of paying that Departure's supplier costs, not as a reduction of them.  
- If it expires unused, the loss is recorded at the agency level, not against the original Departure.

**What we need to know.**

- Is it acceptable to count the credit as a recovery when issued, or should it be recognized only when used?  
- How should an expired credit be recorded?

### **5\. Travel credits that expire unused (CMN-45)**

**Situation.** When the agency cancels a trip, a client may accept a travel credit instead of a refund. The credit belongs to the client who paid and may carry an expiry date.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** It flags credits approaching expiry and records the expiry, but takes no position on what happens to the money.

**What we need to know.**

- May the agency keep an expired, unused travel credit as income?  
- Do unclaimed-property rules, or consumer rules on credits and vouchers, limit expiry or require the amount to be reported or returned?  
- Should travel credits carry an expiry date at all?

## **For the legal adviser**

### **6\. Audit history after an agency account closes (ACC-36)**

**Situation.** DepartureDesk keeps an audit trail of every business change, including financial changes and access to passport details.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** It keeps audit history for the life of the agency account.

**What we need to know.** How long must audit history be kept after an agency stops using DepartureDesk, and how should it then be deleted?

### **7\. Traveler and passport data (CLT-39)**

**Situation.** DepartureDesk stores travelers' dates of birth, passport details and optional passport images, and copies of what was submitted to suppliers.

**What DepartureDesk does now.** Staff can delete passport details and images at any time, with the deletion audited. There are no automatic retention periods yet.

**What we need to know.**

- How long may traveler and passport data be kept after travel?  
- Should it be deleted or anonymized automatically, and does that differ for travelers from the EU or UK?

Each answer becomes a decision log entry. The affected rule moves from Review to Agreed, or is rewritten to match, and any new setting is added to the agency defaults.

## **Working end-to-end journey**

1. **Start a Departure in Draft.** Staff enter the identity, dates, service time zone, client currency, and responsible team. They can save it before every detail is known.  
     
2. **Record supplier arrangements.** For each service, staff capture the supplier, dates, terms, costs, deadlines, and any group benefits. They choose the service’s inventory pattern—unit shells, a capacity pool, or direct supplier booking. Supplier terms and capacity stay separate from client prices.  
     
3. **Prepare the offer.** Staff connect services into a package or standalone offering, set choices, prices, and client terms, and review the details. They decide when the Departure is ready to sell, then explicitly publish the offer to a reseller channel. The system doesn’t make the viability decision for them.  
     
4. **Create the client trip and booking.** Staff or an assigned reseller create a Client Trip, add the responsible client and travelers, and select the offering and any add-ons. The trip may contain several bookings, and it may exist without a Departure. Traveler and supplier details can be completed later when the next step requires them.  
     
5. **Hold space and record client acceptance.** Staff or the reseller place a hold against shared inventory where appropriate, review the booking’s current price and terms, and may share an optional client summary. The client accepts the current price and terms by paying a designated deposit only when the terms say payment is acceptance; otherwise, staff or the reseller record how and when the client accepted. Confirmation snapshots the accepted price and terms. Payment is not otherwise required for confirmation. A hold reserves agency space but does not mean the supplier accepted a reservation.  
     
6. **Complete supplier follow-up.** When a supplier request is needed, staff record it and its response separately from the client booking. A client booking may be confirmed while awaiting a supplier response, but that service must remain clearly marked as awaiting supplier confirmation. Deadline tasks guide submissions, payments, and other follow-up; they don’t automatically change inventory or money records.  
     
7. **Manage money and service changes.** Staff record client charges and payments, supplier obligations and payments, and commission as separate facts. The Client Trip shows one consolidated balance. For a change or cancellation, staff review the client, supplier, inventory, and money effects, then record the amendment or cancellation while preserving the original history.  
     
8. **Complete travel and close out.** Staff handle travel-period issues, finish supplier and client financial follow-up, and reconcile the Departure. The operating trip can be complete even while financial items remain open; those items should stay visible for follow-up.

### **Cruise group terms in the workflow**

Compare two Alpha Cruises arrangements for the same November 6–13, 2027 sailing: eight cabins each in V01, O01, I01, and S01 (32 shells total), with the same occupancy and rate schedule. Treat the terms below as scenario data; staff record the supplier’s actual program and terms rather than having the application infer them from cabin count.  
Deposit-required version: record the \$50 per-stateroom initial deposit for unallocated space, the \$500 per-stateroom full deposit and its actual due trigger, and the final-payment deadline. Create the related supplier obligation and follow-up task at the stated scope. Recording or paying a supplier deposit does not create a client allocation, confirm a client booking, or change cabin capacity.  
Option/courtesy version: mark the same cabin shells conditional through the supplier’s cutoff and show the required staff follow-up. Do not assume an initial deposit is due on unallocated space; record a deposit for named or allocated cabins only if the supplier terms require it. At the cutoff, staff record the supplier response and explicitly extend, convert, or release inventory. The application does not release space automatically.  
For either version, staff or an authorized reseller create a client allocation only through Hold space. The allocation consumes cabin capacity; client acceptance, supplier reservation status, and supplier payment remain separate. An allocation against option/courtesy space stays conditional and displays its cutoff. The \$50/\$500 amounts and March 1 date are illustrative until the actual group program, deposit credit, cutoff meaning, and final-payment date are confirmed.

## **Low-friction rules for the workflow**

- Save partial work and don’t require details before they’re needed.  
- Reuse information already entered; avoid asking staff to restate it in client summaries, supplier submissions, receipts, or reports.  
- Show the next useful action and explain what blocks it.  
- Keep supplier requests, client acceptance, inventory allocation, and payment activity distinct, but present related actions together.  
- Let staff handle verbal and in-person acceptance in the same flow as written acceptance.  
- Preview consequential changes before saving; don’t silently alter accepted prices, terms, allocations, or financial history.

The same spine should cover two variants: **standalone trip** starts with the client and uses direct service bookings without a placeholder Departure; **reseller booking** uses the published offer and assigned booking, while supplier and financial actions remain staff-controlled.

The first workflow check should use a realistic booking with a shared supplier unit, such as a family booking a cruise cabin, plus an optional service. That will test holds, shared occupancy, partial traveler selections, supplier follow-up, and the consolidated trip balance in one pass.

## **Overall assessment**

The Product Specification has a strong model for managing supplier commitments, inventory, client bookings, and money. Its core principles fit the revised goal: one-off Client Trips are supported, client and supplier records stay distinct, data can be entered in stages, integrations are optional, and the agency makes the operating decision.

The main risk is scope, not a missing domain model. The specification makes detailed decisions across many areas. Its status key distinguishes agreed, proposed and deferred product behavior; rules still need a first-release boundary so detailed target behavior is not mistaken for MVP work.

That matters given the earlier goal of starting with practical departure and trip management, including confirmations, invoices and receipts, and simple profitability. The spec’s financial, reporting, document, and operational workflows go substantially further.

| **Medium** | **Several assignment concepts may be hard to explain.** Booking creator, owner, seller of record, reseller relationship, and Departure team serve different purposes (ACC-09, ACC-18, FIN-05). | Keep the distinctions where they affect access or money, but present them with clear defaults and only show the relevant assignment on each workflow. |

## **What the specification already does well for low friction**

Several decisions are particularly aligned with the goal and should be protected:

- Staff can save work before a complete supplier or traveler record exists; holds may use placeholders (CLT-15).  
- A booking can be confirmed without payment (BKG-46).  
- A Client Trip can exist without a Departure, and a service can be planned without shared inventory (OVR-02, BKG-01–02).  
- Supplier integrations are not required; requests and responses can be recorded manually (OVR-03).  
- A deadline creates a task, but does not automatically release inventory, submit information, or pay a supplier (SUP-23–25).  
- Client balances are consolidated while the underlying bookings, suppliers, and collection routes remain distinct (CMN-06).  
- The agency chooses whether to proceed, postpone, or cancel; the system does not make a viability decision (OVR-04, DEP-16).

These are meaningful friction controls. The next step is to ensure that the interface makes them visible—for example, that a draft can be saved, missing details are presented as a next step, and an action is blocked only when the missing information is actually needed.

## **Recommended review outcome**

I would not simplify the product model by collapsing distinctions that protect client money, inventory, or supplier commitments. I would simplify **what must be configured and completed in each workflow**, and stage the less common operational cases.

Before treating the spec as an implementation plan, I recommend adding three things:

1. **A first-release boundary** identifying which agreed rules are essential to operate a real Departure and Client Trip.  
2. **A minimum workflow set**: create a Departure and supplier arrangement; add inventory or a direct reservation; create and confirm a client booking; record client and supplier money; handle a basic change or cancellation; issue a confirmation and receipt; see what needs attention.  
3. **Friction checks for each walkthrough**: can staff save partial work, avoid re-entering known information, understand what blocks the next action, and complete the common case without configuring uncommon features?

My conclusion: **the specification is a strong product-design foundation, but it currently reads more like a broad target system than a minimum viable operating product.** A scope pass and workflow-based friction review should come before implementation planning.
